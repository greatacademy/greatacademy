# Chinese Curriculum

> English | [简体中文](chinese-curriculum.zh-CN.md)

This document specifies the design of the Chinese subject: its sources, its audiences, its knowledge graph, its courses for native, heritage and foreign-language learners, the size of each course in topics, knowledge points and problems, how it teaches and assesses characters, reading, writing, classical texts and speech, and what the core needs for it. It applies the method of [The Math Academy Way](the-math-academy-way.md), runs on the core described in [Content](content.md) and [Learning Engine](learning-engine.md), and reuses what [English Curriculum](english-curriculum.md) built: themed units, passages, recordings, judged writing and the voice service. The first slice of §15, native grades 3 to 6, is built; §15 gives the order of work.

---

## 1. Principles

1. **One subject, one graph.** Native, heritage and foreign-language learners share one graph, as in English. Courses are views over it, each with its own foundations and mastery floor ([Content §6](content.md#6-courses)).
2. **Follow the national standards.** Native courses follow the Ministry of Education's curriculum standards for compulsory and senior secondary Chinese; foreign-language courses follow the national standard for international Chinese education (§2). Where a standard gives a number, such as characters known by the end of grade 2, the course meets it.
3. **The character is the unit of literacy.** Chinese is read and written in characters, which are learned by their parts. Recognizing a character and writing it are separate skills, taught at different rates, as the standards require (§5).
4. **Courses follow levels, not skills.** A grade or a level teaches characters, words, grammar, reading, writing, listening and speaking together in themed units (§8).
5. **Memorized texts are reviewed like everything else.** The standards require the recitation of 135 classical poems and passages in grades 1 to 9 and 72 in senior secondary school. Spaced repetition suits them better than anything else in the subject (§7).
6. **Deterministic judgment wherever possible.** Rules judge characters, pinyin, words, grammar, punctuation and recited lines. AI judges only open writing and speech, after calibration (§11).
7. **Never copy textbooks.** Passages are original or in the public domain in every first market; textbook texts, exercises and word lists are never copied (§13).

## 3. Audiences

| | Native learners | Heritage learners | Foreign-language learners |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Who | Children of families that follow mainland schooling abroad, and adults | Children who speak Chinese at home but read little, common in the United States, Canada, Australia and Singapore | Learners of Chinese as a foreign language, children and adults |
| Already have | Speech in Mandarin, a large spoken vocabulary | Speech, often good listening, sometimes a dialect | Often literacy in another language, if adults |
| Main gaps | Characters, written language, classical texts, writing | Characters and written language; spoken vocabulary narrower than a native child's | Tones, speech, characters, vocabulary size, grammar |
| Courses | Grades 1 to 12 (§8.1) | Literacy bridges that join the grade or level courses (§8.2) | Levels 1 to 9 (§8.3) |
| Explanations in | Chinese | Chinese, with English available | The interface language; Chinese from level 4 |

Heritage learners are named separately, which English does not need: the diagnostic finds their listening far ahead of their reading, and the graph lets them learn characters for words they already say, without the beginner's dialogues of a foreign-language course.

Mainland China is closed to minors by an earlier decision ([Minors' Data](minors-data.md)), so native courses serve families abroad and adults. Hong Kong and Taiwan teach in traditional characters with their own curricula; those curricula are not followed here, and traditional characters are a later variant of the character strand (§5.4).

## 4. What Differs from English and Mathematics

| Idea | In Chinese |
| :--- | :--- |
| Knowledge graph and frontier | Strong order in pinyin, strokes, components and characters: a character's components come before it. Weaker order in reading and literature, as in English |
| Vocabulary in batches | Applies twice: characters and words are both learned in batches (§5, §6) |
| Spaced repetition | Characters, words and recited texts. Recitation is where it is strongest (§7) |
| Fractional implicit repetition | A word encompasses its characters, a recited poem its characters, a composed sentence its grammar |
| Automaticity | Character recognition and reading speed: the standard asks for 300 characters a minute of silent reading by grade 6 and 500 by grade 9 |
| New input | Handwriting by strokes, pinyin with tones, typed Chinese (§10) |
| Reading coverage | Text has no spaces, so coverage is counted in characters and segmented words (§9) |
| Classical language | A strand of its own, from grade 3 to the end of school (§7) |

## 5. Characters

### 5.1 Recognizing and writing

The standard sets cumulative targets, which the grade courses meet:

| End of | Characters recognized | Characters written | Recited texts in the stage | Outside reading in the stage |
| :--- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Grade 2 | about 1,600 | about 800 | 50 | 50,000 characters |
| Grade 4 | about 2,500 | about 1,600 | 50 | 400,000 characters |
| Grade 6 | about 3,000 | about 2,500 | 60 | 1,000,000 characters |
| Grade 9 | about 3,500 | (writing is to be correct and neat; no further number) | 80 | 2,600,000 characters |

Recognizing and writing are separate topics. A **recognition topic** is a batch of 12 characters: it links each one's form to its sound and to words the learner knows, which is how a child who speaks the language learns to read. A **writing topic** is a batch of 10 characters that the learner already recognizes: stroke order, the parts in their places, and writing the character in a word from its pinyin. A writing topic has its recognition topic as a prerequisite.

Foreign-language learners need meaning as well as sound, so their **character topics** link form, sound and meaning in batches of 10, ordered by the levels of GF 0025-2021. Writing topics are shared by all audiences: writing a character is the same skill whoever writes it.

### 5.2 Order: components first

The standard asks that the 300 basic characters of its appendix 4 be learned first: they are simple, frequent and most of them are parts of other characters. The graph does the same. Strokes and stroke order come first; then the basic characters; then the common components and radicals; then characters built from known parts, grouped where possible by a shared part, such as 河、湖、海、洋 by 氵. A character's components are prerequisites of its batch, so the frontier never asks for 想 before 相 and 心.

Until component data is chosen (§5.3), the recognition batches of grades 3 to 6 follow the order of the tables of appendix 5: 12 consecutive characters a batch, dealt in turn to the two grades of a stage, each batch a prerequisite of the grade's next. Of table one, the 900 characters that grades 1 and 2 do not teach go to grades 3 and 4; of table two, 500 go to grades 5 and 6. Reviewers who teach Chinese check the split.

### 5.3 Data

Each character carries its standard form, its readings, its components, its stroke count and stroke order, its tier in the *Table of General Standard Chinese Characters* and its levels in the two standards. This is a subject's data, like the lexicon of English (§12). Stroke geometry for the handwriting checker comes from a data set whose license allows our use, to be chosen and recorded before handwriting is built (§13).

### 5.4 Traditional characters

The first courses are in simplified characters, the national standard. Traditional characters matter in Hong Kong and Taiwan, two of the first markets, but the mapping is not one to one (发 is both 發 and 髮), so converting a course is not a display setting. A later variant adds traditional recognition topics alongside the simplified ones, with their own data; it is decided once the simplified courses are in use.

## 6. Words, Grammar and Punctuation

- **Words** come in batches of 10 by theme or by a shared morpheme, as in English. Idioms (成语) come in batches of 8 from grade 3, with their meaning, their usual errors and their use in a sentence.
- **Grammar for native learners** makes explicit what they already use, within the list of the standard's appendix 3: twelve word classes, five phrase structures, six sentence parts, seven kinds of complex sentence, twelve punctuation marks and eight rhetorical devices. It is taught through use: correcting faulty sentences (病句), changing sentence forms and choosing connectives, as the standard asks, not by defining terms.
- **Grammar for foreign-language learners** follows the grammar points of GF 0025-2021, 572 in all: measure words, aspect with 了, 过 and 着, complements, 把 and 被, and so on. Most are one topic each; a few small points share one.
- **Punctuation** follows GB/T 15834-2011, including the marks English does not have, such as 顿号 and 书名号.

## 7. Classical Texts and Recitation

The compulsory standard recommends 135 classical poems and passages to recite, 75 in grades 1 to 6 and 60 in grades 7 to 9; the senior secondary standard recommends 72 more, 32 in classical prose and 40 poems, songs and lyrics. All of them are in the public domain.

- **One topic per recited text.** Its knowledge points ask for a line from the one before or after it, a line from a description of its meaning, and the correct writing of a line among versions with a wrong character. The review is the same retrieval, spaced by the engine, which is exactly what recitation needs. A text read aloud from memory is a spoken knowledge point, skipped without a microphone.
- **Distinct problems.** A short poem gives fewer than 30 distinct cues. These knowledge points are therefore an exception to the depth required of other rule-checked points, as the listening topics of English are: their items grow only with the text.
- **Classical language** (文言) has topics of its own from grade 5: content words in batches, the 18 common function words, sentence patterns such as the judgment, the passive, omission and inversion, ancient and modern meanings, borrowed characters and translation into modern Chinese. Passages are classical texts in the public domain.

## 8. Courses

A course is a grade or a level. Its units are themes, each with characters, words, grammar, reading, writing, listening and speaking (§8.4), and the engine chooses from the frontier as in English: themes wrap the content, they do not set its order.

### 8.1 Native Courses

One course per grade, `chinese-grade-1` to `chinese-grade-9`, and two for senior secondary school, `chinese-hs-required` and `chinese-hs-selective`, following the required and selective required courses of the senior standard. The elective task groups and a Gaokao course follow (§8.5).

| Course | What weighs most |
| :--- | :--- |
| Grades 1 and 2 | Characters first, then pinyin as a tool, as the 2024 revision of the national textbooks does; strokes and components; 1,600 characters recognized and 800 written; nursery rhymes and short poems; writing a sentence, then a few |
| Grades 3 and 4 | 2,500 and 1,600 characters; the dictionary; words and idioms; paragraphs; narrative and expository reading; poems |
| Grades 5 and 6 | 3,000 and 2,500 characters; reading speed; the first classical passages; whole books; compositions of 400 to 500 characters |
| Grades 7 to 9 | 3,500 characters and semi-cursive writing; classical prose; argument and exposition; three whole books a year; a composition of 500 characters in 45 minutes |
| Senior secondary | The 18 task groups: whole-book reading, language, literature, critical reading, practical reading, classical texts, revolutionary and modern literature, foreign literature, scientific and cultural works |

Grades 1 and 2 are courses of their own, larger than in English because the standard asks for 1,600 characters by the end of grade 2 and Chinese takes a fifth of school time. Their lessons stay short, for a child learning with an adult nearby. There is no preschool course: the guidelines for children aged 3 to 6 ask for listening, speaking and readiness, not characters.

### 8.2 Heritage Courses

Three literacy bridges, `chinese-heritage-1` to `chinese-heritage-3`, take a learner who speaks Chinese from no reading to about 600, 1,500 and 2,500 characters. They are views over the native graph: the same recognition and writing topics, ordered for an older learner, with explanations in Chinese or English and passages written for their age. Their own topics are few: written forms of words used only in speech, the differences between a dialect and Mandarin, and register. After a bridge, the diagnostic places the learner in the grade course that matches their reading, or in levels 5 to 9.

### 8.3 Foreign-Language Courses

Levels follow GF 0025-2021, `chinese-level-1` to `chinese-level-9`. The standard keeps levels 7 to 9 together; the subject splits their lists into three courses of equal size by frequency, so that no course is three times the size of the others.

| Level | Syllables | Characters | Words | Grammar points | Handwritten characters |
| :--- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 269 | 300 | 500 | 48 | 100 |
| 2 | 199 (468) | 300 (600) | 772 (1,272) | 81 (129) | 100 (200) |
| 3 | 140 (608) | 300 (900) | 973 (2,245) | 81 (210) | 100 (300) |
| 4 | 116 (724) | 300 (1,200) | 1,000 (3,245) | 76 (286) | 100 (400) |
| 5 | 98 (822) | 300 (1,500) | 1,071 (4,316) | 71 (357) | 150 (550) |
| 6 | 86 (908) | 300 (1,800) | 1,140 (5,456) | 67 (424) | 150 (700) |
| 7 to 9 | 202 (1,110) | 1,200 (3,000) | 5,636 (11,092) | 148 (572) | 500 (1,200) |

New items per level, with the cumulative total in brackets. Level 1 begins with tones and the sounds a speaker of English or Japanese confuses, heard and said from recordings, then pinyin, then characters; the first characters are basic ones, as for native learners (§5.2). Practice follows the four strands of Nation, as in English.

### 8.4 Units

| Part | In a unit on the market | Judged by |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Characters | A recognition batch and a writing batch (§5) | Rules |
| Words | A batch of 10: 买、卖、斤、便宜、贵 … | Rules |
| Grammar | One or two points the theme uses: measure words, 多少 and 几 | Rules |
| Reading | Graded passages on the theme (§9) | Rules |
| Listening | The unit's passages and dialogues heard without their text | Rules |
| Writing | Sentences, then a paragraph on the theme | Rules, then AI |
| Speaking | Saying the unit's words and sentences, reading aloud | Speech services |
| Conversation | A spoken scene, such as buying fruit at a stall | AI against a rubric |

Native units are knowledge units and genres; heritage and foreign-language units are themes of daily life, then of society and culture.

### 8.5 Exam Courses

Practice-only courses over topics already in the graph, with exam topics of their own for the question types: `chinese-hsk-1` to `chinese-hsk-6` and `chinese-hsk-7-9` for the new HSK, and `chinese-gaokao` for the university entrance examination, whose basis is level 4 of the senior standard. They follow the courses they test.

## 9. Reading Volume

Reading tasks work as in English: graded passages, offered when enough of their characters and words are known, in knowledge units, with a short check. Two things change.

- **Coverage is counted in characters and words.** A passage declares the characters and words it uses; the share of known running characters and of known segmented words are both computed, and a passage is offered when both reach their targets. The targets are parameters to calibrate from the pilot, as English's 98% is.
- **Segmentation is deterministic.** Passages are segmented at compile time by the lexicon, longest match first, with manual breaks where that is wrong. Segmentation is content, not a model, so it is the same everywhere.

The standard's outside reading, from 50,000 characters in grades 1 and 2 to 2,600,000 in grades 7 to 9, is far more than the subject can write. Passages cover the part that needs grading; whole books are read outside the app, from public-domain works hosted in the app and from the standard's recommended books in print, with a topic per book that checks it with our own questions.

## 10. Input and Checkers

`libs/subject-chinese` holds the checkers, pure like the others.

| Checker | Accepts |
| :--- | :--- |
| Choice and order | As in English |
| Pinyin | Tone marks or tone numbers, ü as v, spaces and apostrophes by GB/T 16159; the original tone is written, not the changed tone of sandhi |
| Characters | Typed Chinese compared after normalization; a variant form counts as wrong where the standard form is taught |
| Strokes | Handwriting as a sequence of strokes on screen, matched stroke by stroke against the standard order and shapes; each wrong stroke is reported where it happened |
| Recited lines | Exact characters and punctuation of the line, so that a wrong character is the error it is |
| Speech | Pronunciation assessment for zh-CN, with tones; as in English, intelligibility is judged, not accent, and low confidence asks for another recording |

The web and the apps need a handwriting pad and a pinyin keyboard with tones; typed Chinese uses the device's input method.

## 11. Writing and Speaking

The levels of English apply, with character counts instead of word counts.

| Level | In Chinese | Judged by |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| W1 Conventions | Wrong characters, punctuation, pinyin | Rules |
| W2 Sentences | Expanding and combining sentences, changing sentence forms, connectives | Rules for form, AI for meaning |
| W3 Revision | Correcting faulty sentences, ordering sentences, choosing the better word | Mostly rules |
| W4 Paragraphs | One move at a time | AI, against yes-or-no criteria |
| W5 Compositions | 记叙文, 说明文, 议论文 and practical writing, from 300 characters in grade 3 to the 800-character essay of the Gaokao | AI, against a rubric |

Judged writing needs calibration sets scored by teachers of Chinese, separate from those of English. Speaking follows S1 to S6 of English: listening to tones comes first for foreign-language learners; native learners practice reading aloud and presenting, not pronunciation, and a regional accent costs nothing.

## 12. Content Model and Core Changes

The core stays subject-agnostic, but three of its assumptions are English's.

- **Tokenizing text.** `bodyWords` and `spokenWords` in `libs/content` split on letters and spaces, so a run of Chinese characters counts as one word. The core must take the subject's tokenizer: characters and segmented words for Chinese, as now for English.
- **Characters as data.** A subject may define characters, as English defines a lexicon: form, readings, components, strokes, tier and levels. The compiler checks that a batch's components are taught before it.
- **New inputs.** `strokes` and `pinyin` join the input kinds, with their checkers in `libs/subject-chinese` and their pads in the apps.

Everything else is reused: `t(en, zh)` with Chinese being learned written in both locales the same, item banks, passages, recordings, judged writing, conversations and the voice service. The engine needs no change: character and word batches are topics, and if the pilot shows that characters within a batch are forgotten at very different rates, the engine gains item-level memory then, as was decided for English words.

## 13. Sources of Content and Rights

- **Standards** are cited and summarized, never reproduced.
- **Character lists.** The *Table of General Standard Chinese Characters* was issued by the State Council; whether it, the lists of GF 0025-2021 and the appendices of the curriculum standards may be used as data is a question for counsel before content is written, as E0.5 asked for English's word sources.
- **Dictionary data.** CC-CEDICT is licensed CC BY-SA, which would make derived glosses share-alike, so glosses are written for the subject instead. Unihan, under the Unicode license, may supply readings and radicals.
- **Stroke data** for the handwriting checker needs a license that allows use in a paid product; candidates are reviewed and the choice recorded before handwriting is built.
- **Texts.** Classical texts are in the public domain. A modern text is used only if it is in the public domain in every first market, checked per work; otherwise passages are written for the subject. Textbook texts and exercises are never copied.
- **Reference repository.** `cn-math-taxonomy` was read for orientation. It is licensed CC BY-NC 4.0, which forbids commercial use, so none of its data, questions or wording is taken.

## 15. Order of Work

1. **Decisions first:** counsel on character lists and stroke data (§13); a reviewer who teaches Chinese in primary school and one who teaches it as a foreign language; the core change for tokenizing (§12).
2. **First slice: native grades 3 to 6 without new input.** Recognition batches, words, idioms, punctuation, faulty sentences, recited texts and passages, all checked by choices and typed Chinese. It tests whether mastery and spaced recitation work in Chinese, and needs neither handwriting nor speech. Built: four courses, `chinese-grade-3` to `chinese-grade-6`, with 208 topics and 486 knowledge points (117 recognition batches, 16 word batches, 16 idiom batches, 19 grammar topics of punctuation, faulty sentences, connectives, sentence forms and rhetoric, 32 recited poems, and in grades 5 and 6 six recited classical passages and two drills of classical words) and 36 passages in 12 reading units, served by `apps/chinese-workers`; the reviews and the counsel of step 1 remain.
3. **Foreign-language levels 1 to 3**, with recordings made ahead of time as for English, and the pinyin checker. Begun: the course `chinese-pinyin`, 6 topics of tones, initials, finals, spelling rules and tone changes that need no audio.
4. **Handwriting:** the stroke pad in the web and the apps, the stroke checker, and the writing topics.
5. **Grades 1 and 2** and the heritage bridges, which need handwriting, pinyin and recordings.
6. **Grades 7 to 9, senior secondary and classical language.**
7. **Levels 4 to 9, judged writing, speech and conversations, then the exam courses.**

[Chinese Development Plan](chinese-development-plan.md) breaks this list down into milestones, files and gates.

## 16. Risks and Questions

- **Volume.** Some 4,200 topics are as much content as mathematics. Character and word batches are regular enough to generate from data, which is where tooling matters most.
- **Handwriting.** Judging strokes fairly for children's writing on a phone is new to the platform and needs testing on real devices with real children.
- **Tones and speech services.** Pronunciation assessment for Mandarin tones has to be tested on learners whose first language is English, Japanese and Korean, and on children.
- **Rights.** The character lists and stroke data may limit or delay what can be built (§13).
- **Fit with schools.** Families abroad may want the textbook's order, which the graph does not follow. The grade courses cover each grade's standard, not its textbook.
- **Exam dates.** The new HSK's dates come from secondary sources and are confirmed with the test owner first.
