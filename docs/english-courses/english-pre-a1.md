# English Pre-A1

> This is Great Academy's own outline. Its design is in the [English curriculum](../english-curriculum.md) §6.2. [简体中文](english-pre-a1.zh-CN.md).

- **Course:** `english-pre-a1`
- **Level:** CEFR Pre-A1, for learners of English as a foreign language
- **Prerequisite(s):** None: the course starts from the alphabet and the first words
- **Language of explanation:** The interface language: Chinese or English
- **Size:** 6 units · 30 topics (15 of vocabulary, 12 of grammar, 3 of writing) · 164 headwords, 164 by the end of the level

## Aim

By the end of Pre-A1 a learner understands and uses about 160 of the commonest English words: greetings, the numbers to twelve, colors, family, the classroom, animals and the body, and the function words that hold sentences together (the, a, I, you, is, and, in, this, have, can, what...). They read and write short sentences with *be*, *have* and *can*, ask and answer *What is it?* and *Is it a cat?*, and follow simple classroom commands. This is below A1 in the CEFR, as in the Cambridge Pre A1 Starters.

Explanations are short and lean on examples. For Chinese speakers they name the points where English differs from Chinese: *he* and *she* where Chinese says tā, the verb *be* that Chinese often leaves out, *a* and *an* that Chinese does not have, and the plural *-s*.

## How to Read This Outline

Each unit lists its topics by strand. A vocabulary topic teaches its batch of headwords; the spellings in parentheses are the forms a passage of this level or a higher one may also use. Every word is taught once in all levels, and a passage uses only words taught at its level or below. Grammar and writing topics list their prerequisites; a topic marked *existing* is already in the graph and keeps its own prerequisites. Writing topics are judged by rules or by a model (§8). Each unit closes with the reading unit and the conversation named below; listening and speaking wait for audio and recording, and each unit says what it will gain.

## Units

### 1. Hello!

**Vocabulary**

- `pre-a1-words-hello` Hello and Goodbye (11): hello, hi, goodbye, bye, name (names), yes, no, please, thank (thanks, thanked, thanking), sorry, friend (friends)
- `pre-a1-words-i-and-you` I, You and We (10): I, you, he, she, it, we, they, am (i'm), is (isn't, it's, he's, she's), are (aren't, you're, we're, they're)

**Grammar**

- `the-verb-be` Am, Is and Are (after `pre-a1-words-i-and-you`). *I am*, *you are*, *she is*: English needs the verb *be* where Chinese often has no verb (*I am happy*, not *I happy*).

**Reading:** `pre-a1-reading-hello`, 6 passages: Two children meet on the first day of school; A girl says her name and asks for a friend's; Saying goodbye at the school gate; A new friend in class; Thank you and sorry; Hello from a boy and his friend.

**Conversation:** `meeting-a-new-classmate`. The tutor is a new classmate on the first day of school. Task: Say hello, say your name, ask the classmate's name, and say goodbye.

**Later, with audio and recording:** Listening to greetings and names, and telling *he* from *she*; saying *hello*, *goodbye* and *My name is...* aloud.

### 2. Numbers and Colors

**Vocabulary**

- `pre-a1-words-numbers` Numbers 1 to 12 (12): one, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine, ten, eleven, twelve
- `pre-a1-words-colors` Colors (12): color (colors), red, blue, green, yellow, black, white, orange (oranges), pink, brown, purple, gray
- `pre-a1-words-how-many` What, How Many, How Old (10): what (what's), how, many, old (older, oldest), year (years), a, an, the, and, not

**Grammar**

- `a-and-an` A and An (after `pre-a1-words-how-many`). One thing takes *a* or *an*: *a pen*, *an egg*. Chinese has no such word, so learners often leave it out (*It is pen*).
- `plural-nouns-with-s` One Cat, Two Cats (after `pre-a1-words-numbers`, `a-and-an`). More than one takes *-s* or *-es*: *two cats*, *three boxes*; Chinese marks number with a measure word, not on the noun.

**Writing**

- `writing-capitals-and-periods` Capital Letters and Periods (judged by rules; after `the-verb-be`). A sentence begins with a capital letter and ends with a period or a question mark, and *I* is always a capital.

**Reading:** `pre-a1-reading-numbers-and-colors`, 6 passages: Twelve oranges, counted again; My colored pencils; How old is Sam?; Red, blue and green bags; Twelve children in a classroom; What color is it? (riddles).

**Conversation:** `counting-and-colors`. The tutor sells toys at a market stall. Task: Say which toy you want by its color, and ask how many there are.

**Later, with audio and recording:** Listening for numbers and colors in short sentences; saying the numbers to twelve and the colors.

### 3. My Family

**Vocabulary**

- `pre-a1-words-family` Family (11): family (families), mother (mothers), father (fathers), mom (moms), dad (dads), sister (sisters), brother (brothers), baby (babies), grandma (grandmas), grandpa (grandpas), who (who's)
- `pre-a1-words-my-and-your` My, Your, Me and You (11): my, your, his, her, its, our, their, me, him, us, them
- `pre-a1-words-people` People (10): man (men), woman (women), boy (boys), girl (girls), child (children), people, have (has, had, having, haven't, hasn't), love (loves, loved, loving), with, too

**Grammar**

- `he-she-and-it` He, She and It (after `pre-a1-words-family`, `the-verb-be`). *He* is for a man or boy, *she* for a woman or girl, *it* for a thing or an animal. Spoken Chinese says tā for all three, so learners mix them up.
- `my-your-his-her` My, Your, His and Her (after `pre-a1-words-my-and-your`, `he-she-and-it`). *my mom*, *his sister*, *their dog*: the word for whose comes before the noun and takes no *de*. *His* is for a man, *her* for a woman.
- `have-and-has` Have and Has (after `pre-a1-words-people`, `he-she-and-it`). *I have*, *you have*, but *she has*, *he has*, *it has*.

**Reading:** `pre-a1-reading-my-family`, 6 passages: Who is in my family?; A baby brother; Grandma and Grandpa are here; Who is that man?; Two sisters and a brother; I love my family.

**Conversation:** `talking-about-my-family`. The tutor is a friend looking at your family photo. Task: Say who is in your family, and give their names and ages.

**Later, with audio and recording:** Listening for *he* and *she* in sentences about a family; saying family words and *This is my...* aloud.

### 4. My Classroom

**Vocabulary**

- `pre-a1-words-classroom` The Classroom (11): book (books), pen (pens), pencil (pencils), bag (bags), desk (desks), chair (chairs), ruler (rulers), eraser (erasers), teacher (teachers), school (schools), classroom (classrooms)
- `pre-a1-words-this-and-that` This, That, Here and There (10): this, that (that's), these, those, here, there (there's), in, on, under, where (where's)
- `pre-a1-words-classroom-actions` Classroom Actions (10): open (opens, opened, opening), close (closes, closed, closing), sit (sits, sat, sitting), stand (stands, stood, standing), look (looks, looked, looking), listen (listens, listened, listening), read (reads, reading), write (writes, wrote, written, writing), draw (draws, drew, drawn, drawing), do (does, did, done, doing, don't, doesn't, didn't)

**Grammar**

- `this-that-these-those` This, That, These and Those (after `pre-a1-words-this-and-that`, `plural-nouns-with-s`). *This* and *these* are near, *that* and *those* are far; *these* and *those* are for more than one.
- `in-on-and-under` In, On and Under (after `pre-a1-words-this-and-that`, `pre-a1-words-classroom`). *The pen is in the bag.* The place word comes before the thing, not after it as in Chinese (zài bāo lǐ).
- `imperatives` Commands (after `pre-a1-words-classroom-actions`). *Open your book. Don't stand up.* A command starts with the verb and has no *you*.

**Writing**

- `writing-words-in-order` Words in Order (judged by rules; after `writing-capitals-and-periods`). Put words in the order of an English sentence: who, then the verb, then the rest.

**Reading:** `pre-a1-reading-classroom`, 6 passages: What is in my bag?; Our classroom; The teacher says: open your books; Where is my pen?; These books and those books; My school.

**Conversation:** `finding-things-in-the-classroom`. The tutor is a teacher who cannot find things in the classroom. Task: Say where each thing is, with *in*, *on* and *under*.

**Later, with audio and recording:** Following spoken classroom commands; saying classroom words and *It's on the desk.*

### 5. Animals

**Vocabulary**

- `pre-a1-words-animals` Animals (12): animal (animals), cat (cats), dog (dogs), bird (birds), fish, horse (horses), cow (cows), pig (pigs), duck (ducks), rabbit (rabbits), mouse (mice), frog (frogs)
- `pre-a1-words-can-and-like` Can, Like and Actions (11): can (can't, cannot), like (likes, liked, liking), see (sees, saw, seen, seeing), run (runs, ran, running), jump (jumps, jumped, jumping), swim (swims, swam, swum, swimming), fly (flies, flew, flown, flying), walk (walks, walked, walking), eat (eats, ate, eaten, eating), big (bigger, biggest), small (smaller, smallest)
- `pre-a1-words-little-words` Little Words (11): but, or, very, to, at, of, good (better, best), bad (worse, worst), happy (happier, happiest), sad (sadder, saddest), long (longer, longest)

**Grammar**

- `can-for-ability` Can and Can't (after `pre-a1-words-can-and-like`). *A bird can fly. A fish can't walk.* After *can* the verb has no *-s* and no *to*.
- `questions-with-be` Questions with Be (after `the-verb-be`, `pre-a1-words-animals`). *Is it a cat? Yes, it is.* The verb moves to the front; Chinese adds *ma* at the end instead.

**Reading:** `pre-a1-reading-animals`, 6 passages: Grandpa's animals; A cat and a mouse; Can a fish fly?; A big dog and a small dog; What animals do you like?; Hop the frog.

**Conversation:** `my-favorite-animal`. The tutor is a friend at the zoo gate. Task: Say which animal you like, what color it is and what it can do.

**Later, with audio and recording:** Listening to animal riddles (*It is big. It can swim.*) and choosing the animal; saying what an animal can do.

### 6. My Body

**Vocabulary**

- `pre-a1-words-body` The Body (12): body (bodies), head (heads), face (faces), eye (eyes), ear (ears), nose (noses), mouth (mouths), hand (hands), arm (arms), leg (legs), foot (feet), hair

**Grammar**

- `adjectives-before-nouns` Big Eyes, Long Hair (after `pre-a1-words-body`, `pre-a1-words-colors`, `have-and-has`). A describing word comes before the noun and never takes *-s*: *two big eyes*, not *two bigs eyes*.

**Writing**

- `writing-about-yourself` Writing About Yourself (judged by rules; after `writing-words-in-order`, `have-and-has`). Write true sentences about yourself from notes: your name, your age and what you have.

**Reading:** `pre-a1-reading-my-body`, 6 passages: My face; A monster with three eyes; Hands and feet; Long hair and big eyes; What can your body do?; A happy face and a sad face.

**Conversation:** `drawing-a-monster`. The tutor draws a monster from what you say. Task: Say how many heads, eyes and legs it has, and what color its hair is.

**Later, with audio and recording:** Listening to *Touch your nose* and pointing; saying body words.
