# English B2

> This is Great Academy's own outline. Its design is in the [English curriculum](../english-curriculum.md) §6.2. [简体中文](english-b2.zh-CN.md).

- **Course:** `english-b2`
- **Level:** CEFR B2, for learners of English as a foreign language
- **Prerequisite(s):** B1, or the tenses, conditionals, the passive and reported speech, and about 2,200 words
- **Language of explanation:** The interface language: Chinese or English
- **Size:** 10 units · 139 topics (110 of vocabulary, 16 of grammar, 13 of writing) · 1320 headwords, 3543 by the end of the level

## Aim

By the end of B2 a learner understands the main ideas of complex texts on both concrete and abstract topics, can interact with a degree of fluency and spontaneity that makes regular talk with native speakers possible without strain, and can produce clear, detailed text on a wide range of subjects, explaining a viewpoint on a topical issue with the advantages and disadvantages of various options (CEFR B2). They know about 3,500 words, read general texts of 250 to 450 words fluently, and write essays, reports, proposals and letters. After B2, learners continue in the native secondary and college courses (§6.2).

The grammar finishes the conditionals and modals (the third and mixed conditionals, *wish*, deduction and past modals), extends the passive and the future, and adds the structures of formal writing: reporting verbs, cleft sentences, inversion and linking words of contrast. Writing reuses the essay topics of the native courses (thesis statements, introductions and conclusions, evidence and counterclaim paragraphs, revision) and adds the genres of the exams and of work, judged by a model.

## How to Read This Outline

Each unit lists its topics by strand. A vocabulary topic teaches its batch of headwords; the spellings in parentheses are the forms a passage of this level or a higher one may also use. Every word is taught once in all levels, and a passage uses only words taught at its level or below. Grammar and writing topics list their prerequisites; a topic marked *existing* is already in the graph and keeps its own prerequisites. Writing topics are judged by rules or by a model (§8). The reading unit and the conversation are written from this outline in a later step; listening and speaking wait for audio and recording, and each unit says what it will gain.
