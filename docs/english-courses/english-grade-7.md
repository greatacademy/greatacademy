# English, Grade 7

> This is Great Academy's own outline. Its design is in the [English curriculum](../english-curriculum.md) §6.3 and the [development plan](../english-development-plan.md) §E8. [简体中文](english-grade-7.zh-CN.md).

- **Course:** `english-grade-7`
- **Level:** Grade 7, for learners whose first language is English or who read English as well as a native speaker of their age
- **Prerequisite(s):** `english-grade-6`, or reading and writing explanations, comparisons, memoirs and letters at the level of grade 6
- **Language of explanation:** English
- **Size:** 10 units · 102 topics (16 existing, 86 new: 38 of vocabulary, 14 of grammar and mechanics, 17 of reading, 10 of writing checked by rules, 7 of writing judged by a model) · about 1,510 XP · 9 reading units of 74 passages (2 existing, 7 new) · 10 conversations (2 existing, 8 new)

## Aim

By the end of grade 7 a learner reads the histories, science texts, speeches, stories and news of the grade on their own and weighs what they read. In arguments they find the claim, the evidence and the reasoning, answer a counterclaim, judge whether evidence is relevant and sufficient, see how a speaker builds a case and notice loaded language; in texts of facts they cite evidence, keep a summary objective, follow how people, events and ideas interact, trace a scientific idea over time, read historical maps, graphs and diagrams, tell correlation from cause and an observation from an inference, and see how the sections of a text work together. In stories they follow how setting, plot and character shape one another, contrast points of view, hear what dialogue reveals and compare a story with the history it draws on. They use active and passive voice and the moods of verbs without shifting them, phrases and clauses, adjective and adverb clauses, the past perfect, the subjunctive in demands, pronouns in pairs and verbs that agree with tricky subjects; they place modifiers so none dangles, punctuate interrupters and join clauses with semicolons, and spell words with suffixes and the plurals of Latin and Greek. They write a thesis, an outline and the paragraphs of an argument, cut wordiness and redundancy, organize by category, use details of a period, report numbers from a graph, explain with an analogy, pace a story with sentence length and write a headline and lead; and they write an informative essay, historical fiction, a cause-and-effect essay, a report with data, a lab conclusion, a suspense story, a five-paragraph argument and a persuasive speech. They know the words of ten themes: argument and the law, the oceans, the Renaissance, the empires of West Africa, disease and medicine, money and business, imagined futures, rights and reform, flight, and the news.

## How to Read This Outline

Every unit is a theme and teaches four things together: the words of the theme, grammar and mechanics, reading skills, and writing. It ends with a reading unit on the theme and a conversation, except the last, which has a conversation but no reading unit. Topics marked *existing* are in the graph already, in the band course `english-grades-6-8`, and keep their ids and their own prerequisites; each new topic lists its prerequisites, which may lie in an earlier unit or an earlier grade. A new topic of words lists the words it teaches, none of which another batch of the native courses teaches; any other new topic says what it teaches and its knowledge points as steps. A judged writing topic names its task and the criteria of its rubric, marking the criteria that are new.

Strands: of the 102 topics, 40 teach words (39%), 17 grammar and mechanics (17%), 22 reading (22%), 11 writing checked by rules (11%) and 12 writing judged by a model (12%).

Depth: each new grammar, reading or rule-checked writing knowledge point has 30 to 48 items built from hand-written cores by templates; each word of a vocabulary batch appears in three sentences, so each vocabulary knowledge point has 30 items; each judged writing knowledge point has 12 tasks with a model; new reading units have 8 passages of 200 to 400 words, with three questions each. A batch of ten words earns about 15 XP, a word-study, grammar, reading or rule-checked writing topic about 14 and a judged writing topic about 22, and the existing topics keep their own XP, so the lessons of the grade come to about 1,510 XP.

Listening and speaking wait for audio and recording: until then a unit lists its conversation but does not offer it, and each unit says what it will gain.

The band course `english-grades-6-8` retires once `english-grade-6`, `english-grade-7` and `english-grade-8` hold all of its topics. Its 55 topics are divided among the three grades by its units, each in exactly one, with its id unchanged: 24 in grade 6 (myths, poetry and Rome), 16 in grade 7 (argument and oceans) and 15 in grade 8 (turning points, machines and exploration). Its reading units and conversations go with their units.

### 9. Flight

Steps: The Meaning of the Greek root; then Choosing the Word from Its Parts.

**Grammar:** `spelling-words-with-suffixes` Spelling Words with Suffixes (after `spelling-doubling-final-consonants`): A final silent e drops before a suffix that starts with a vowel, and a final y after a consonant becomes i. Steps: Dropping or Keeping a Final E; then Changing Y to I.

**Reading:** `integrating-text-and-diagrams` Integrating Text and Diagrams (after `text-features-in-science`): Diagrams, flow charts and tables carry information that words alone carry poorly; good readers move between the text and the visual. Steps: Which Visual Helps; then Text or Visual.

**Writing:** `explaining-with-an-analogy` Explaining with an Analogy (after `comparisons-that-explain`): An analogy explains something new by comparing it with something familiar that works the same way, and a good one matches the feature that matters. Steps: The Analogy That Fits; then Judging an Analogy.

### 10. News and Media

**Grammar:** `commonly-confused-words-3` Commonly Confused Words 3 (after `commonly-confused-words-2`): Pairs such as principal and principle, imply and infer, or farther and further sound alike or close, but mean different things. Steps: Words That Sound Alike; then Words Close in Meaning or Form.

**Reading:** `advertising-techniques` Advertising Techniques (after `loaded-language`, `authors-purpose`): Ads persuade with techniques such as the bandwagon, a famous face, snob appeal and fear, and they mix checkable facts with vague claims and opinions. Steps: Naming the Technique; then Fact or Claim in an Ad.

**Writing:** `writing-a-headline-and-lead` Writing a Headline and Lead (after `reading-a-news-article`): A news headline names the main event in a few strong words without opinion, and the lead gives the most important facts first: who, what, when and where. Steps: The Best Headline; then The Best Lead.

**Conversation:** `news-editor-headline` Fixing a headline with the editor *(new)*. Task: You write for the school news website. Your editor thinks your headline and opening are unfair. Discuss what makes them loaded, rewrite them in neutral words and explain how a reader can tell news from an advertisement.

**Later, with audio and recording:** Listening to a news report and an advertisement and telling them apart; reading a news lead aloud.

## What the Next Grade Builds On

`english-grade-8` builds on this year: the literary analysis paragraph grows from comparing points of view and story elements, and irony, foreshadowing and symbolism join the reader's tools; the five-paragraph argument and the informative essay go on to comparing texts on one topic, introductions and conclusions and revising a draft; grammar moves to gerund and infinitive phrases, the kinds of sentences, and dashes, parentheses and ellipses; and the themes of flight and the Renaissance go on to the machines that changed the world and the voyages of exploration.
