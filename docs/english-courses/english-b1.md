# English B1

> This is Great Academy's own outline. Its design is in the [English curriculum](../english-curriculum.md) §6.2. [简体中文](english-b1.zh-CN.md).

- **Course:** `english-b1`
- **Level:** CEFR B1, for learners of English as a foreign language
- **Prerequisite(s):** A2, or the past simple and continuous, the future with *will* and *going to*, the present perfect, and about 1,200 words
- **Language of explanation:** The interface language: Chinese or English
- **Size:** 12 units · 119 topics (83 of vocabulary, 18 of grammar, 18 of writing) · 994 headwords, 2261 by the end of the level

## Aim

By the end of B1 a learner understands the main points of clear standard texts on familiar matters met at school, at work and in leisure, can deal with most situations likely to arise while travelling, can produce simple connected text on familiar topics, and can describe experiences, events, hopes and ambitions and briefly give reasons and explanations for opinions and plans (CEFR B1). They know about 2,200 words, read texts of 180 to 350 words, and write paragraphs, emails formal and informal, stories, articles and reviews.

The grammar completes the tenses most used in speech and writing (the present perfect against the past simple, the present perfect continuous, the past perfect, *used to*) and adds conditionals, the passive, reported speech, relative clauses, gerunds and infinitives, and question forms. Writing reuses the paragraph topics of the native courses (topic sentences, transitions, run-ons), which suit learners at this level, and adds texts of everyday life judged by a model.

## How to Read This Outline

Each unit lists its topics by strand. A vocabulary topic teaches its batch of headwords; the spellings in parentheses are the forms a passage of this level or a higher one may also use. Every word is taught once in all levels, and a passage uses only words taught at its level or below. Grammar and writing topics list their prerequisites; a topic marked *existing* is already in the graph and keeps its own prerequisites. Writing topics are judged by rules or by a model (§8). The reading unit and the conversation are written from this outline in a later step; listening and speaking wait for audio and recording, and each unit says what it will gain.
