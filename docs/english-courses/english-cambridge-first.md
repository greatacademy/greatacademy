# Cambridge B2 First

> This is Great Academy's own outline. Its design is in the [English curriculum](../english-curriculum.md) §6.2. [简体中文](english-cambridge-first.zh-CN.md).

- **Course:** `english-cambridge-first`, in the group English Exams
- **Level:** CEFR B2, for learners of English as a foreign language preparing for B2 First
- **Prerequisite(s):** English B2 (`english-b2`)
- **Language of explanation:** The interface language: Chinese or English
- **Size:** 3 units · 45 topics (7 new, all practice-only) · 1 reading unit of 6 passages

## The Test

Checked on 2026-11-10 against the official description at [cambridgeenglish.org: B2 First exam format](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/exams-and-tests/qualifications/first/format/).

| Paper | Format | In this course |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Reading and Use of English | 1 hour 15 minutes, 7 parts, 52 questions. Part 1: multiple-choice cloze (8); Part 2: open cloze (8); Part 3: word formation (8); Part 4: key word transformations (6); Part 5: multiple choice on a long text (6); Part 6: gapped text (6); Part 7: multiple matching (10) | Units 1 and 2; each item of Parts 1 to 4 is one gap in a sentence or two |
| Writing | 1 hour 20 minutes. Part 1: a compulsory essay of 140 to 190 words, using two given ideas and one of the writer's own. Part 2: one of an article, an email or letter, a report or a review, 140 to 190 words | Unit 3 |
| Listening, Speaking | Four parts of recordings; an interview, a long turn, a collaborative task and a discussion, in pairs | Not yet: see below |

Questions, texts and word lists of the test are never copied: every text and item here is written for this course in the formats above.

## Aim

Use of English rewards exact knowledge of collocations, phrasal verbs, word families and grammar in context, and the course practices each part in its own format. Reading practices the long text with multiple-choice questions, the gapped text and matching across four short texts, and Writing the essay and the four texts of Part 2 at the test's length. It teaches nothing new: its own topics are practice-only, and the topics it reuses keep their place in the graph.

## How to Read This Outline

Each unit lists the topics it reuses (*existing*) and its new topics. A new topic judged by rules has two knowledge points of rising difficulty with at least 30 items each; one judged by a model has 12 tasks with model answers. Passages use only words of B2 and below, apart from names, and run to about 300 to 500 words, within the length the exam courses allow; the texts of the test are somewhat longer. Each item of Parts 1 to 4 is one gap in a sentence or two rather than a text of eight gaps, so that every item has its own explanation.

## Units

### 1. Use of English

**Existing:** `phrasal-verbs`, `idioms`, `more-idioms`, `b2-words-linking-1`, `b2-words-linking-2`, `b2-words-personality-3`, `b2-words-strong-feelings`, `common-prefixes`, `suffixes-that-change-word-class`, `articles-in-general-and-specific`, `relative-clauses`, `prepositions-before-relative-pronouns`, `gerunds-and-infinitives`, `used-to`, `so-and-such`, `reported-speech`, `third-conditional`, `passive-with-modals-and-perfect-forms`, `causative-have-and-get`, `reporting-verbs`, `modals-of-deduction`, `past-modals`, `mixed-conditionals`, `wish-and-if-only`, in three modules: words in context (Parts 1 and 3), grammar words (Part 2) and key word transformations (Part 4).

**New**

- `fce-multiple-choice-cloze` Multiple-Choice Cloze (practice-only; after `phrasal-verbs`). Choose the one word of four that fits a gap: (1) collocations and fixed phrases; (2) phrasal verbs, linking words and words followed by a particular preposition or pattern.
- `fce-open-cloze` Open Cloze (practice-only; after `relative-clauses`, `articles-in-general-and-specific`). Type the one word a gap needs: (1) articles, prepositions, auxiliaries and pronouns; (2) linking words, relative words, quantifiers and parts of fixed phrases.
- `fce-word-formation` Word Formation (practice-only; after `b2-words-linking-1`, `suffixes-that-change-word-class`, `common-prefixes`). Type the form of a given word that fits a gap: (1) nouns, adjectives and verbs from a stem; (2) negative prefixes, adverbs and plurals.
- `fce-key-word-transformations` Key Word Transformations (practice-only; after `third-conditional`, `reported-speech`, `passive-with-modals-and-perfect-forms`). Complete a second sentence with two to five words, including a given word, so that it means the same as the first: (1) passives, comparisons and verb patterns; (2) conditionals, reported speech, past modals and phrasal verbs.

**Later, with audio and recording:** nothing; Use of English is read and written only.

### 2. Reading

**Existing:** `making-inferences`, `authors-purpose`, `tone-and-mood`, `b2-words-tone`, `ordering-sentences-in-a-paragraph`, `choosing-transitions`.

**New**

- `fce-multiple-matching` Multiple Matching (practice-only; after `making-inferences`). Eight sets of four people, A to D, writing on one subject, each with eight statements: (1) what a person did or what happened to them; (2) how a person felt or what they think, said in other words.

**Reading:** `fce-reading`, 6 passages of 300 to 500 words with 36 questions in the formats of Parts 5 to 7. Multiple choice on a long text (Part 5), six questions each: A year without a phone; The woman who mapped the ocean floor. Gapped text (Part 6), five gaps each, with one sentence that fits no gap: Learning to cook at forty; A theatre that saved a town. Multiple matching (Part 7), seven questions each across four short texts: Four young volunteers; Night shift at the zoo.

**Later, with audio and recording:** the four parts of Listening; the four parts of Speaking, with the tutor as partner and examiner.

### 3. Writing

**Existing:** `writing-an-opinion-essay`, `writing-an-informal-email`, `writing-a-formal-email`, `writing-an-article`, `b2-words-film-and-tv`, `b2-words-tourism`, `writing-a-review`, `writing-a-report`.

**New**

- `fce-writing-an-essay` Writing Part 1: the Essay (judged by a model; practice-only; after `writing-an-opinion-essay`). An essay of 140 to 190 words for a teacher that answers the question, uses both given notes and adds an idea of the writer's own.
- `fce-writing-part-2` Writing Part 2 (judged by a model; practice-only; after `writing-an-article`, `writing-a-review`, `writing-a-report`). An article, an email or letter, a report or a review of 140 to 190 words, in the register its reader expects; three tasks of each kind.
