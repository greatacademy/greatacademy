# English, Grade 3

> This is Great Academy's own outline. Its design is in the [English curriculum](../english-curriculum.md) §6.3 and the [development plan](../english-development-plan.md) §E8. [简体中文](english-grade-3.zh-CN.md).

- **Course:** `english-grade-3`
- **Level:** Grade 3, for learners whose first language is English or who read English as well as a native speaker of their age
- **Prerequisite(s):** Reading simple stories and writing simple sentences
- **Language of explanation:** English
- **Size:** 10 units · 116 topics (45 existing, 71 new: 34 of vocabulary, 8 of grammar and mechanics, 15 of reading, 9 of writing checked by rules, 5 of writing judged by a model) · about 1,570 XP · 10 reading units of 90 passages (5 existing, 5 new) · 10 conversations (5 existing, 5 new)

## Aim

By the end of grade 3 a learner reads stories, poems and informational texts of the grade on their own. They retell a story's key events, name its setting, problem and solution, describe its characters and how they change, and state the lesson of a fable or folk tale; in a text of facts they find the main idea and the details that support it, use text features, tables and timelines, and follow the order of a procedure. They write complete sentences of four kinds with correct capitals and end marks, plurals, possessives and contractions, the simple tenses with agreement, and compound and complex sentences joined with conjunctions; they punctuate dialogue, dates, addresses and letters. They write a paragraph with a topic sentence, details in order, transitions and a closing sentence, and from it an opinion, a how-to paragraph, a paragraph of facts, a friendly letter, a fable and a short poem. They know the words of ten themes: fables and folk tales, plants, animals, weather, maps, ancient Egypt, ancient China, forces and magnets, music and poetry, and how base words, prefixes, suffixes and compounds build words.

## How to Read This Outline

Every unit is a theme and teaches four things together: the words of the theme, grammar and mechanics, reading skills, and writing. It ends with a reading unit on the theme and a conversation. Topics marked *existing* are in the graph already, in the band course `english-grades-3-5`, and keep their ids and their own prerequisites; each new topic lists its prerequisites, which may lie in an earlier unit or an earlier grade. A new topic of words lists the words it teaches, none of which another batch of the native courses teaches; any other new topic says what it teaches and its knowledge points as steps. A judged writing topic names its task and the criteria of its rubric, marking the criteria that are new.

Strands: of the 116 topics, 45 teach words (39%), 23 grammar and mechanics (20%), 24 reading (21%), 15 writing checked by rules (13%) and 9 writing judged by a model (8%).

Depth: each new grammar, reading or rule-checked writing knowledge point has 30 to 48 items built from hand-written cores by templates; each word of a vocabulary batch appears in three sentences, so each vocabulary knowledge point has 30 items; each judged writing knowledge point has 12 tasks with a model; reading units have 8 to 10 passages of 140 to 260 words, with three questions each. A batch of ten words earns about 15 XP, a word-study, grammar, reading or rule-checked writing topic about 14 and a judged writing topic about 22, so the lessons of the grade come to about 1,570 XP.

Listening, speaking and phonics wait for audio and recording: until then a unit lists its conversation but does not offer it, and each unit says what it will gain.

The band course `english-grades-3-5` retires once `english-grade-3`, `english-grade-4` and `english-grade-5` hold all of its topics. Its 78 topics are divided among the three grades, each in exactly one, with its id unchanged: 45 in grade 3, 21 in grade 4 and 12 in grade 5.

Steps: naming the part of a text (chapter, line, stanza); then choosing what a given part adds to the whole. `what-a-poem-is-about` What a Poem Is About (after `parts-of-stories-and-poems`, `making-inferences`): A poem has a subject and a feeling, and its pictures in words show both. Steps: choosing what a short poem is about; then choosing the lines that show the poem's feeling.

**Writing:** `strong-verbs` Strong Verbs (after `action-and-linking-verbs`): A strong verb shows exactly how something moves or sounds (*the leaves skittered* rather than *the leaves went*). Steps: choosing the verb that fits a meaning given in brackets; then replacing a weak verb in a sentence. `writing-a-seasons-poem` Writing a Poem About a Season (judged by a model; after `what-a-poem-is-about`, `strong-verbs`): A poem of six to ten lines about a season, with pictures for at least two senses and a pattern of repetition or rhyme. Criteria: `g3-poem-subject` *(new)*, `g35-describe-senses`, `g3-poem-pattern` *(new)*.

**Reading unit:** `poems-for-all-seasons` Poems for All Seasons *(new)*, 8 passages: First Snow; The Puddle Jumper; Spring Is Late; Bees in the Clover; The Hottest Day; Fireflies; The Leaf Pile; The Last Apple.

**Conversation:** `poem-for-a-grandparent` Sharing a poem with a grandparent *(new)*. The tutor is a grandparent on a video call. Task: share a short poem about a season, say what it is about and which words make the best pictures.

**Later, with audio and recording:** Listening to poems read aloud and hearing their rhythm and rhyme; reciting a short poem from memory.

## What the Next Grade Builds On

Grade 4 builds on this year: the paragraph grows into reports of two paragraphs with headings, persuasive letters, summaries and stories with dialogue and description; grammar moves to the progressive tenses, modals, prepositional phrases, the order of adjectives and run-ons; reading turns to whole text structures, firsthand and secondhand accounts, plays and figurative language; and the themes of Egypt and China lead on to the Middle Ages and the Silk Road, the weather to the biomes of the world, and forces to energy.
