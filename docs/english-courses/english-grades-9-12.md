# English, Grades 9 to 12

> This is Great Academy's own outline. Its design is in the [English curriculum](../english-curriculum.md) §11. [简体中文](english-grades-9-12.zh-CN.md).

- **Course:** `english-grades-9-12`
- **Level:** Grades 9 to 12, for learners whose first language is English or who read English as well as a native speaker of their age
- **Prerequisite(s):** [English, Grade 8](english-grade-8.md) (`buildsOn: ["english-grade-8"]`)
- **Language of explanation:** English
- **Size:** 8 units · 37 topics (10 existing, 27 new: 8 of vocabulary, 8 of grammar and mechanics, 4 of reading, 7 of writing judged by a model) · 8 reading units (2 existing, 6 new) · 8 conversations

## Aim

By the end of grade 12 a learner reads literature, drama, poetry, speeches, historical documents and writing about science with full understanding of how they are made: their rhetorical situation and appeals, their structure and style, their allusions and forms. They weigh sources and fallacies, and synthesize several sources into one argument. They write with control of the sentence: semicolons and colons, absolute phrases, periodic and cumulative sentences, sentences varied for effect. They write whole essays of the kinds that school and university ask for: literary and rhetorical analysis, argument, explanation, analysis of a poem, a scene of drama, a personal essay and a college application essay.

## How to Read This Outline

Every unit is a theme and teaches four things together: the words of the theme, grammar and style, a reading skill, and writing. It ends with a reading unit on the theme and a conversation. Topics marked *existing* are in the graph already and keep their own prerequisites; each new topic lists its prerequisites, and topics of grades 3 to 8 count as known. Every existing topic of grades 9 to 12 is in exactly one unit.

Depth: each new rule-checked knowledge point has at least 30 items built from hand-written cores by templates; each word of a vocabulary batch appears in three sentences; each judged writing knowledge point has at least 12 tasks with a model, and each judged topic is one knowledge point, the whole piece; each new reading unit has 8 passages of about 300 to 340 words, within the bounds of 300 to 600, with three questions each. The existing topics of this band have one or two knowledge points; their banks are deepened to 30 items, or 12 tasks when judged, when the course is built, keeping their ids.

Listening and speaking wait for audio and recording: until then a unit lists its conversation but does not offer it, and each unit says what it will gain.

## Units

### 1. The Writer's Craft

**Words:** `words-for-literary-analysis` Words for Literary Analysis (after `context-clues`, `theme`): ambiguity, motif, juxtaposition, archetype, protagonist, antagonist, catharsis, epiphany, satire, allegory.

**Grammar:** `semicolons-and-colons` Semicolons and Colons (after `run-ons-and-comma-splices`, `appositives`).

**Reading:** `characterization`, `allusion` *(existing)*.

**Writing:** `writing-a-literary-analysis-essay` Writing a Literary Analysis Essay (judged by a model; after `writing-a-literary-analysis-paragraph`, `introductions-and-conclusions`, `characterization`): a thesis about a story's meaning, body paragraphs with quoted evidence, and a conclusion.

**Reading unit:** `the-writers-craft` *(existing)*, 10 passages.

**Conversation:** `seminar-on-a-short-story`. The tutor leads a seminar. Task: state an interpretation of a story, support it with a detail, and answer a classmate's challenge.

**Later, with audio and recording:** Listening to a recorded seminar and noting each speaker's claim; leading a discussion aloud.

### 2. Voices of Persuasion

**Words:** `words-of-rhetoric` Words of Rhetoric (after `context-clues`, `authors-purpose`): rhetoric, anecdote, concession, analogy, euphemism, inflammatory, rebuttal, polemic, eloquent, demagogue.

**Grammar:** `periodic-and-cumulative-sentences` Periodic and Cumulative Sentences (after `participial-phrases`, `parallel-structure`).

**Reading:** `rhetorical-situation`, `rhetorical-appeals`, `rhetorical-devices` *(existing)*.

**Writing:** `writing-a-rhetorical-analysis` *(existing)*.

**Reading unit:** `voices-of-persuasion` *(existing)*, 10 passages.

**Conversation:** `speech-coaching-session`. The tutor is a speech coach. Task: present the opening of a speech you plan to give, and revise it after the coach's question about your audience.

**Later, with audio and recording:** Listening to famous speeches and naming their appeals and devices; delivering a short speech aloud.

### 3. Tragedy and the Stage

**Words:** `words-of-the-theater` Words of the Theater (after `context-clues`): playwright, soliloquy, monologue, aside, chorus, tragedy, hubris, farce, understudy, denouement.

**Grammar:** `conditional-sentences` Conditional Sentences (after `verb-moods`): real, unreal and mixed conditionals, and *had I known…*.

**Reading:** `dramatic-structure-and-dialogue` Dramatic Structure and Dialogue (after `irony`): acts and scenes, stage directions, dramatic irony and what dialogue reveals. `paradox-and-oxymoron` *(existing)*.

**Writing:** `writing-a-scene` Writing a Scene (judged by a model; after `writing-a-narrative-from-a-point-of-view`, `dramatic-structure-and-dialogue`): a short scene in script form, with a conflict carried by dialogue and stage directions.

**Reading unit:** `tragedy-on-the-stage` *(new)*, 8 passages, original essays, a story and a scene: the theater of Athens; Shakespeare's Globe; the shape of a tragedy; the chorus; an understudy's opening night; farce; reading stage directions; a scene at a bus stop.

**Conversation:** `director-casting-discussion`. The tutor is a director casting a play. Task: argue which actor should play the lead, using what the script reveals about the character.

**Later, with audio and recording:** Listening to a recorded scene and noting how voice shows character; performing a monologue.

### 4. Rights and Laws

**Words:** `words-of-government-and-law` Words of Government and Law (after `context-clues`): constitution, amendment, legislature, judiciary, verdict, jury, petition, suffrage, statute, precedent.

**Grammar:** `absolute-phrases` Absolute Phrases (after `participial-phrases`).

**Reading:** `logical-fallacies` *(existing)*.

**Writing:** `writing-an-argumentative-essay` Writing an Argumentative Essay (judged by a model; after `writing-a-counterclaim-paragraph`, `introductions-and-conclusions`, `logical-fallacies`): a full argument with a thesis, evidence, a counterclaim and its rebuttal.

**Reading unit:** `rights-and-laws` *(new)*, 8 passages: the laws of Hammurabi; the Magna Carta; how a jury decides; the right to vote; the three branches of government; a school mock trial; fallacies in court; a student's petition.

**Conversation:** `mock-trial-witness`. The tutor is a lawyer in a school mock trial. Task: as a witness, tell what you saw clearly and stay consistent under cross-examination.

**Later, with audio and recording:** Listening to an oral argument and naming each side's strongest point and any fallacy; arguing a case aloud.

### 5. Science and Society

**Words:** `words-of-scientific-inquiry` Words of Scientific Inquiry (after `context-clues`): hypothesis, variable, replicate, empirical, correlation, anomaly, consensus, ethical, placebo, skeptical.

**Grammar:** `noun-clauses` Noun Clauses (after `relative-clauses`): clauses with *that*, *whether* and *wh-* words as subjects and objects.

**Reading:** `evaluating-sources` *(existing)*.

**Writing:** `synthesizing-sources` *(existing)*.

**Reading unit:** `science-and-society` *(new)*, 8 passages: Semmelweis and hand washing; correlation and causation; placebos and controlled trials; replication; reading a science headline; the hole in the ozone layer; editing genes; a fair test at a science fair.

**Conversation:** `science-fair-judge-questions`. The tutor judges a science fair. Task: explain your experiment, its variables and what your results do and do not show.

**Later, with audio and recording:** Listening to a science news report and judging how well its claims are supported; presenting an experiment aloud.

### 6. A Century of Change

**Words:** `words-of-modern-history` Words of Modern History (after `context-clues`): industrialization, immigration, depression, propaganda, totalitarian, alliance, decolonization, globalization, refugee, revolution.

**Grammar:** `integrating-quotations` Integrating Quotations (after `citing-textual-evidence`, `punctuating-dialogue`): introducing, punctuating, shortening with ellipses and adjusting with brackets; ellipses are explained but not tested.

**Reading:** `analyzing-historical-documents` Analyzing Historical Documents (after `primary-and-secondary-sources`, `rhetorical-situation`).

**Writing:** `writing-an-explanatory-essay` Writing an Explanatory Essay (judged by a model; after `writing-an-informative-paragraph`, `introductions-and-conclusions`, `analyzing-historical-documents`).

**Reading unit:** `a-century-of-change` *(new)*, 8 passages: the mill girls of Lowell; Ellis Island; an invented letter from the front, read as a primary source; the crash of 1929; wartime posters and propaganda; the end of empire in India; the fall of the Berlin Wall; globalization.

**Conversation:** `oral-history-interview`. The tutor plays an elderly neighbor who lived through great changes. Task: interview them about one event and find out what they saw and how it changed their life.

**Later, with audio and recording:** Listening to recorded oral histories and comparing them with a textbook account; conducting an interview aloud.

### 7. Poems Across Time

**Words:** `words-for-poetic-form` Words for Poetic Form (after `context-clues`, `sound-devices`): stanza, couplet, sonnet, ballad, haiku, ode, elegy, meter, refrain, enjambment.

**Grammar:** `word-order-for-emphasis` Word Order for Emphasis (after `simple-compound-and-complex-sentences`): inversion, fronting and the end of the sentence as its place of stress.

**Reading:** `poetic-forms-and-meter` Poetic Forms and Meter (after `sound-devices`).

**Writing:** `writing-a-poetry-analysis` Writing a Poetry Analysis (judged by a model; after `responding-to-a-poem`, `poetic-forms-and-meter`, `writing-a-literary-analysis-essay`).

**Reading unit:** `poems-across-time` *(new)*, 8 passages, each explaining a form with an original poem: the sonnet; the ballad; the haiku; the elegy; the ode; the line break and free verse; meter; two poems on one river compared.

**Conversation:** `poetry-workshop-feedback`. The tutor is a fellow poet in a workshop. Task: give specific feedback on their poem: one strength and one suggestion, with reasons.

**Later, with audio and recording:** Listening to a poem and hearing its meter; reading a sonnet aloud.

### 8. Essays of Experience

**Words:** `words-for-reflection` Words for Reflection (after `context-clues`): introspective, poignant, candid, ambivalent, formative, resolve, vulnerable, perseverance, gratitude, aspiration.

**Grammar:** `sentence-variety-for-effect` Sentence Variety for Effect (after `periodic-and-cumulative-sentences`, `varying-sentence-openings`).

**Reading:** `reading-personal-essays` Reading Personal Essays (after `narrative-point-of-view`, `authors-purpose`): how an essayist moves between the story of an experience and reflection on it.

**Writing:** `writing-a-personal-essay` Writing a Personal Essay (judged by a model; after `writing-a-narrative-from-a-point-of-view`, `introductions-and-conclusions`, `reading-personal-essays`). `writing-a-college-application-essay` Writing a College Application Essay (judged by a model; after `writing-a-personal-essay`): an essay of at least 200 words, with models of about 300 to 350, in answer to a prompt of the kind universities set.

**Reading unit:** `essays-of-experience` *(new)*, 8 original personal essays and essays about the form: a lost sweater; what a personal essay does; learning a father's language; losing a race; the kitchen table; a year of silence; a letter to a younger self; the night shift at a diner.

**Conversation:** `college-admissions-interview`. The tutor is an admissions interviewer. Task: answer questions about yourself with specific examples, and ask one question of your own about the university.

**Later, with audio and recording:** Listening to an essay read by its author and noting how it reflects; answering interview questions aloud.
