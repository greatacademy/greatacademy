# English, Grades 6 to 8

> This is Great Academy's own outline. Its design is in the [English curriculum](../english-curriculum.md) §11. [简体中文](english-grades-6-8.zh-CN.md).

- **Course:** `english-grades-6-8`
- **Level:** Grades 6 to 8, for learners whose first language is English or who read English as well as a native speaker of their age
- **Prerequisite(s):** [English, Grades 3 to 5](english-grades-3-5.md) (`buildsOn: ["english-grades-3-5"]`)
- **Language of explanation:** English
- **Size:** 8 units · 55 topics (34 existing, 21 new: 8 of vocabulary, 6 of grammar and mechanics, 2 of reading, 5 of writing judged by a model) · 8 reading units (2 existing, 6 new) · 8 conversations

## Aim

By the end of grade 8 a learner analyzes stories, poems and myths: their plot and conflict, theme, narrator, tone, figurative language and symbols, and cites the text to support what they say. They read arguments and informational texts critically, telling a claim from its evidence and reasoning, and compare two texts on one topic. They write complex sentences with clauses, phrases and parallel structure, and whole pieces: an argument with a counterclaim, an informative paragraph, an objective summary, a literary analysis, a retold myth and a narrative from a point of view.

## How to Read This Outline

Every unit is a theme and teaches four things together: the words of the theme, grammar and punctuation, a reading skill, and writing. It ends with a reading unit on the theme and a conversation. Topics marked *existing* are in the graph already and keep their own prerequisites; each new topic lists its prerequisites, and topics of grades 3 to 5 count as known. Every existing topic of grades 6 to 8 is in exactly one unit.

Depth: each new rule-checked knowledge point has at least 30 items built from hand-written cores by templates; each word of a vocabulary batch appears in three sentences; each new judged writing knowledge point has at least 12 tasks with a model; each new reading unit has 8 to 10 passages of 200 to 400 words with three questions each. The existing topics of this band have one or two knowledge points with about ten items each; each of their rule-checked knowledge points is deepened to at least 30 items, keeping their ids; their judged writing topics keep 8 tasks each.

Listening and speaking wait for audio and recording: until then a unit lists its conversation but does not offer it, and each unit says what it will gain.

## Units

### 1. Myths and Legends

**Words:** `words-from-myths` Words from Myths (after `context-clues`): echo, panic, titanic, odyssey, nemesis, chaos, labyrinth, herculean, mentor, siren. `latin-roots-2` *(existing)*.

**Grammar:** `relative-clauses` *(existing)*; `pronoun-case` Pronoun Case (after `pronoun-antecedent-agreement`): *I* or *me*, *who* or *whom*, and pronouns in compound subjects and objects.

**Reading:** `plot-and-conflict`, `theme`, `narrative-point-of-view` *(existing)*.

**Writing:** `combining-with-relative-clauses` *(existing)*; `retelling-a-myth` Retelling a Myth (judged by a model; after `writing-a-narrative-paragraph`, `plot-and-conflict`): a myth retold in two or three paragraphs from notes, keeping its conflict and its lesson.

**Reading unit:** `myths-and-legends` *(new)*, 8 original retellings: Pangu separates the sky and the earth; Prometheus brings fire; Pandora's jar; Icarus and his wings; Arachne the weaver; Persephone and the seasons; Hou Yi and the ten suns; Maui slows the sun.

**Conversation:** `myth-retelling-for-a-podcast`. The tutor hosts a school podcast. Task: retell a myth for the listeners and explain what it tried to explain about the world.

**Later, with audio and recording:** Listening to a storyteller and identifying the conflict and the turning point; retelling a myth aloud.

### 2. Poetry and Song

**Words:** `words-for-tone-and-feeling` Words for Tone and Feeling (after `context-clues`): wistful, somber, jubilant, bleak, serene, ominous, whimsical, nostalgic, melancholy, exuberant. `connotation-and-precise-words` *(existing)*.

**Grammar:** `appositives` *(existing)*; `coordinate-adjectives` Commas Between Adjectives (after `order-of-adjectives`, `commas-in-a-series`): a comma between coordinate adjectives (*a long, cold night*) but none between cumulative ones (*an old stone bridge*).

**Reading:** `personification-and-hyperbole`, `sound-devices`, `tone-and-mood` *(existing)*.

**Writing:** `combining-with-appositives` *(existing)*; `responding-to-a-poem` Responding to a Poem (judged by a model; after `tone-and-mood`, `writing-a-paragraph`): a paragraph on what a poem's images and sounds make the reader feel, quoting it.

**Reading unit:** `tales-and-poems` *(existing)*, 10 passages.

**Conversation:** `poetry-night-introduction`. The tutor runs a poetry evening at a library. Task: introduce a poem you chose, say what it is about and why you like it.

**Later, with audio and recording:** Listening to poems read aloud and hearing rhythm and rhyme; reading a poem aloud with expression.

### 3. Ancient Rome

**Words:** `words-of-ancient-rome` Words of Ancient Rome (after `context-clues`): republic, senate, empire, aqueduct, gladiator, legion, forum, citizen, consul, emperor.

**Grammar:** `participial-phrases`, `misplaced-modifiers` *(existing)*.

**Reading:** `primary-and-secondary-sources` Primary and Secondary Sources (after `authors-purpose`, which already follows `fact-and-opinion`; it also encompasses `fact-and-opinion`): who wrote a source, when, and how close they were to the events.

**Writing:** `varying-sentence-openings` *(existing)*; `writing-an-informative-paragraph` Writing an Informative Paragraph (judged by a model; after `writing-a-paragraph`, `text-structures`): a paragraph that explains a topic from notes with facts, definitions and examples.

**Reading unit:** `ancient-rome` *(new)*, 8 passages: the legend of the founding; the republic; the senate and the consuls; roads; aqueducts; a day in Pompeii; the Colosseum; the end of the empire in the west.

**Conversation:** `roman-forum-tour-guide`. The tutor guides a tour of the ruins of the Forum. Task: ask about one building, what happened there and how we know.

**Later, with audio and recording:** Listening to a tour and noting dates and places; explaining a Latin root aloud.

### 4. Making the Case

**Words:** `words-of-argument` Words of Argument (after `context-clues`, `fact-and-opinion`): assert, justify, valid, bias, perspective, concede, refute, persuade, credible, relevant.

**Grammar:** `active-and-passive-voice` *(existing)*; `verb-moods` Verb Moods (after `modal-verbs`, which already follows `simple-verb-tenses`): indicative, imperative, conditional and subjunctive (*If I were…*, *I suggest that he be…*).

**Reading:** `claims`, `evidence`, `reasoning`, `counterclaims-and-rebuttals` *(existing)*.

**Writing:** `writing-a-thesis-statement`, `outlining-an-argument`, `writing-an-evidence-paragraph`, `writing-a-counterclaim-paragraph` *(existing)*.

**Reading unit:** `making-the-case` *(existing)*, 10 passages.

**Conversation:** `student-council-proposal`. The tutor is the student council president. Task: propose a change at school, give two reasons, and answer one objection.

**Later, with audio and recording:** Listening to two sides of a debate and naming each claim and its evidence; giving a one-minute argument aloud.

### 5. Oceans and Ecosystems

**Words:** `words-of-ecosystems` Words of Ecosystems (after `context-clues`): ecosystem, organism, producer, consumer, decomposer, biodiversity, symbiosis, plankton, coral, endangered.

**Grammar:** `parallel-structure` *(existing)*.

**Reading:** `citing-textual-evidence` *(existing)*.

**Writing:** `cutting-wordiness` *(existing)*; `writing-an-objective-summary` Writing an Objective Summary (judged by a model; after `writing-a-summary`, `citing-textual-evidence`): the central idea and its development in a longer text, without opinions.

**Reading unit:** `life-in-the-ocean` *(new)*, 8 passages: the zones of the ocean; plankton; coral reefs; kelp forests; ocean food webs; the migration of whales; plastic in the sea; protecting the oceans.

**Conversation:** `marine-biologist-interview`. The tutor is a marine biologist on a research boat. Task: find out what she studies, how she studies it and why it matters.

**Later, with audio and recording:** Listening to a science podcast and summarizing it objectively; explaining a food web aloud.

### 6. Turning Points

**Words:** `words-for-character-and-motive` Words for Character and Motive (after `context-clues`, `characters`): resentful, envious, resilient, impulsive, conscientious, defiant, sympathetic, aloof, earnest, remorseful.

**Grammar:** `gerund-and-infinitive-phrases` Gerund and Infinitive Phrases (after `participial-phrases`): verbals as subjects, objects and modifiers, and telling a gerund from a participle.

**Reading:** `irony`, `foreshadowing-and-flashback` *(existing)*.

**Writing:** `writing-a-literary-analysis-paragraph` *(existing)*.

**Reading unit:** `turning-points` *(new)*, 8 original short stories about growing up: a first job; a move to a new city; a lost friendship; a grandparent's illness; a mistake owned up to; a team that loses; a new sibling; a long-awaited trip that goes wrong.

**Conversation:** `book-club-discussion`. The tutor is a member of a book club. Task: discuss a character's turning point in a story you read, and support your view with details.

**Later, with audio and recording:** Listening to a short story read aloud and noticing foreshadowing; discussing a character aloud.

### 7. Machines That Changed the World

**Words:** `words-of-technology` Words of Technology (after `context-clues`): invention, engine, circuit, patent, prototype, efficient, mechanism, automate, innovation, transmit.

**Grammar:** `simple-compound-and-complex-sentences` Simple, Compound and Complex Sentences (after `subordinating-conjunctions`, which already follows `coordinating-conjunctions`; it encompasses both): naming the kind of a sentence and choosing one to show how ideas relate.

**Reading:** `comparing-texts-on-a-topic` Comparing Texts on One Topic (after `citing-textual-evidence`, `compare-and-contrast-in-texts`): how two authors present the same events or facts differently.

**Writing:** `combining-sentences-in-your-own-words`, `introductions-and-conclusions` *(existing)*.

**Reading unit:** `machines-that-changed-the-world` *(new)*, 8 passages: the printing press; the steam engine; the telegraph; the light bulb; the telephone; the airplane; the computer; the smartphone.

**Conversation:** `inventor-pitch`. The tutor is a judge at an invention fair. Task: present an invention, explain the problem it solves and answer one question about it.

**Later, with audio and recording:** Listening to two accounts of one invention and comparing them; pitching an invention aloud.

### 8. Explorers and Voyages

**Words:** `words-of-exploration` Words of Exploration (after `context-clues`): expedition, voyage, navigate, compass, latitude, longitude, colony, frontier, uncharted, perilous.

**Grammar:** `dashes-parentheses-and-ellipses` Dashes, Parentheses and Ellipses (after `appositives`, `commas-after-introductions`).

**Reading:** `symbolism` *(existing)*.

**Writing:** `revising-a-draft` *(existing)*; `writing-a-narrative-from-a-point-of-view` Writing a Narrative from a Point of View (judged by a model; after `writing-a-narrative-paragraph`, `narrative-point-of-view`): an event told by one character, with what that character can know and feel.

**Reading unit:** `explorers-and-voyages` *(new)*, 8 passages: the fleets of Zheng He; Polynesian navigators; Ibn Battuta; the voyage of Columbus and its costs; Magellan's crew sails around the world; the race to the South Pole; the dive to the deepest sea; the first steps on the moon.

**Conversation:** `expedition-planning-meeting`. The tutor leads an expedition team. Task: plan a journey to a place of your choice: the route, what to take and one danger to prepare for.

**Later, with audio and recording:** Listening to an explorer's diary read aloud and following the route on a map; telling a journey aloud from one traveler's point of view.
