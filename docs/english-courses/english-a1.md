# English A1

> This is Great Academy's own outline. Its design is in the [English curriculum](../english-curriculum.md) §6.2. [简体中文](english-a1.zh-CN.md).

- **Course:** `english-a1`
- **Level:** CEFR A1, for learners of English as a foreign language
- **Prerequisite(s):** Pre-A1, or knowing its words and sentences with *be*, *have* and *can*
- **Language of explanation:** The interface language: Chinese or English
- **Size:** 12 units · 73 topics (43 of vocabulary, 19 of grammar, 11 of writing) · 489 headwords, 653 by the end of the level

## Aim

By the end of A1 a learner can understand and use familiar everyday expressions and very basic sentences, introduce themselves and others, and ask and answer questions about where they live, people they know and things they have (CEFR A1). They know about 600 words, read short texts on everyday themes, and write sentences, questions and, from the library unit on, short notes, postcards and descriptions.

The grammar is the present: the present simple with its third-person *-s*, its negatives and questions, and the present continuous; then countable and uncountable nouns, *there is*, prepositions of place and time, and *was* and *were*. These are the points where Chinese speakers most often go wrong: Chinese verbs do not change for the person or the time, questions keep the order of statements, and nouns do not mark one or many.

## How to Read This Outline

Each unit lists its topics by strand. A vocabulary topic teaches its batch of headwords; the spellings in parentheses are the forms a passage of this level or a higher one may also use. Every word is taught once in all levels, and a passage uses only words taught at its level or below. Grammar and writing topics list their prerequisites; a topic marked *existing* is already in the graph and keeps its own prerequisites. Writing topics are judged by rules or by a model (§8). Each unit closes with the reading unit and the conversation named below; listening and speaking wait for audio and recording, and each unit says what it will gain.

## Units

### 1. All About Me

**Vocabulary**

- `a1-words-numbers-to-hundred` Numbers 13 to 100 (12): thirteen, fourteen, fifteen, sixteen, seventeen, eighteen, nineteen, twenty, thirty, forty, fifty, hundred (hundreds)
- `a1-words-about-me` Where I Live (11): country (countries), city (cities), live (lives, lived, living), from, speak (speaks, spoke, spoken, speaking), English, Chinese, language (languages), phone (phones), number (numbers), email (emails)
- `a1-words-feelings` How I Feel (11): feel (feels, felt, feeling), fine, well, okay, great (greater, greatest), tired, sick, angry, afraid, now, again
- `a1-words-everyday-words` Everyday Words (12): thing (things), place (places), person, life, idea (ideas), part (parts), cry (cries, cried, crying), high (higher, highest), low (lower, lowest), sure, ready, real
- `a1-words-little-words-3` Little Words 3 (9): as, let's, each, alone, onto, oh, wow, hey, yeah

**Grammar**

- `present-simple` The Present Simple (after `the-verb-be`, `a1-words-about-me`). *I live in Beijing. We speak Chinese.* The present simple tells what is true or usual; with *I*, *you*, *we* and *they* the verb has no ending.

**Reading:** `a1-reading-all-about-me`, 7 passages: A student from Canada; Pen friends; Languages in our class; How old are you?; A phone number; My city; How do you feel today?.

**Conversation:** `introducing-yourself`. The tutor is a teacher in an online class with students from many countries. Task: Say your name, your age, where you are from and which languages you speak.

**Later, with audio and recording:** Listening to people introduce themselves and noting their age and country; saying numbers to a hundred and introducing yourself.

### 2. My Day

**Vocabulary**

- `a1-words-my-day` My Day (12): get (gets, got, gotten, getting), up, wake (wakes, woke, woken, waking), go (goes, went, gone, going), come (comes, came, coming), home (homes), bed (beds), sleep (sleeps, slept, sleeping), breakfast (breakfasts), lunch (lunches), dinner (dinners), brush (brushes, brushed, brushing)
- `a1-words-times-of-day` Times of Day (10): morning (mornings), afternoon (afternoons), evening (evenings), night (nights), day (days), today, every, then, after, before
- `a1-words-how-often` How Often (11): always, usually, often, sometimes, never, start (starts, started, starting), finish (finishes, finished, finishing), work (works, worked, working), watch (watches, watched, watching), play (plays, played, playing), homework
- `a1-words-little-words-1` Little Words 1 (10): for, out, down, off, over, by, into, just, really, why

**Grammar**

- `third-person-s` He Plays, She Watches (after `present-simple`, `he-she-and-it`). After *he*, *she*, *it* or one person, a verb in the present simple ends in *-s* or *-es*: *she gets up*, *he watches*, *it goes*. Chinese verbs never change, so this *-s* is the most common A1 error.
- `adverbs-of-frequency` Always, Usually, Never (after `present-simple`, `a1-words-how-often`). *I always get up at seven.* These words go before the verb, but after *am*, *is* and *are*: *She is never late.*

**Writing**

- `writing-sentences-about-routines` Writing About Routines (judged by rules; after `third-person-s`, `writing-capitals-and-periods`). Turn notes into sentences about a day: *Lily / get up / 7:00* becomes *Lily gets up at seven o'clock.*

**Reading:** `a1-reading-my-day`, 7 passages: A busy Monday; A farmer's morning; A night nurse; Breakfast with Grandpa; Homework after school; Sunday is different; My dog's day.

**Conversation:** `talking-about-your-day`. The tutor is a friend in another country. Task: Tell your friend when you get up, what you do after school and when you go to bed, and ask about their day.

**Later, with audio and recording:** Listening to someone describe their day and putting the pictures in order; saying your routine with *always* and *never*.

**Vocabulary**

- `a1-words-time` Telling the Time (12): time (times), clock (clocks), o'clock, half (halves), quarter (quarters), past, minute (minutes), hour (hours), week (weeks), weekend (weekends), late, early
- `a1-words-days` Days of the Week (10): Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday, Saturday, Sunday, month (months), next, last
- `a1-words-months` Months (12): January, February, March, April, May, June, July, August, September, October, November, December
- `a1-words-dates` Dates and Birthdays (12): sixty, seventy, eighty, ninety, zero, first, second (seconds), third, fourth, fifth, birthday (birthdays), date (dates)
- `a1-words-ordinals` Sixth to Thirtieth (9): sixth, seventh, eighth, ninth, tenth, eleventh, twelfth, twentieth, thirtieth

**Grammar**

- `prepositions-of-time` At, On and In for Time (after `a1-words-days`, `a1-words-time`). *at* seven o'clock, *on* Monday, *in* May: Chinese uses no word here, or the same word for all three.

**Writing**

- `writing-capital-letters-for-names` Capital Letters for Names (judged by rules; after `writing-capitals-and-periods`, `a1-words-months`). Names of people, places, languages, days and months begin with a capital letter: *On Monday, Anna speaks English.*

**Conversation:** `making-a-plan-to-meet`. The tutor is a friend who wants to meet this week. Task: Agree on a day and a time to meet, and say why other times are no good.

**Later, with audio and recording:** Listening for times and dates in short messages; saying the time and your birthday.

### 4. Food and Drink

**Vocabulary**

- `a1-words-food` Food (12): food (foods), fruit (fruits), vegetable (vegetables), meat (meats), chicken (chickens), rice, bread (breads), egg (eggs), apple (apples), banana (bananas), potato (potatoes), tomato (tomatoes)
- `a1-words-drinks` Drinks (12): water, milk, juice, tea, coffee, cup (cups), glass (glasses), bottle (bottles), drink (drinks, drank, drunk, drinking), hungry (hungrier, hungriest), thirsty, cake (cakes)
- `a1-words-meals` Meals (12): some, any, much, lot (lots), bowl (bowls), plate (plates), spoon (spoons), fork (forks), knife (knives), table (tables), cook (cooks, cooked, cooking), kitchen (kitchens)
- `a1-words-favorite-foods` Favorite Foods (12): sandwich (sandwiches), pizza (pizzas), noodle (noodles), dumpling (dumplings), soup (soups), salad (salads), ice, cream, candy (candies), cookie (cookies), sugar, salt

**Grammar**

- `countable-and-uncountable-nouns` Countable and Uncountable Nouns (after `plural-nouns-with-s`, `a1-words-food`). *an apple, two apples*, but *some rice*, *some water*: some nouns are never counted and take no *-s* and no *a*.
- `some-and-any` Some and Any (after `countable-and-uncountable-nouns`, `a1-words-meals`). *There is some milk. Is there any juice? There isn't any bread.* *Some* in statements, *any* in questions and negatives.

**Writing**

- `joining-with-and-but-or` Joining with And, But and Or (judged by rules; after `writing-words-in-order`). Join two short sentences: *and* adds, *but* shows a difference, *or* gives a choice.

**Reading:** `a1-reading-food`, 7 passages: Breakfast around the world; Making a fruit salad; What's in the kitchen?; A birthday cake; Noodles or rice?; Lunch at school; A hungry rabbit.

**Conversation:** `ordering-lunch`. The tutor is the waiter at a small cafe. Task: Order something to eat and something to drink, and ask for one more thing you need.

**Later, with audio and recording:** Listening to an order in a cafe and choosing the right tray; saying food words and *I'd like...*

### 5. Clothes and Shopping

**Vocabulary**

- `a1-words-clothes` Clothes (12): clothes, shirt (shirts), T-shirt (t-shirts), dress (dresses), skirt (skirts), pants, shoe (shoes), sock (socks), hat (hats), coat (coats), jacket (jackets), wear (wears, wore, worn, wearing)
- `a1-words-shopping` Shopping (12): shop (shops, shopped, shopping), buy (buys, bought, buying), sell (sells, sold, selling), money, dollar (dollars), cheap (cheaper, cheapest), expensive, price (prices), store (stores), pay (pays, paid, paying), size (sizes), try (tries, tried, trying)
- `a1-words-at-the-shop` Which One? (11): want (wants, wanted, wanting), would, new (newer, newest), another, other (others), same, different, only, all, more, most

**Grammar**

- `present-continuous` The Present Continuous (after `the-verb-be`, `a1-words-clothes`). *She is wearing a red coat.* What is happening now takes *am*, *is* or *are* and a verb with *-ing*; Chinese uses *zhèng zài* and no change to the verb.
- `how-much-and-how-many` How Much and How Many (after `countable-and-uncountable-nouns`, `a1-words-shopping`). *How many apples? How much milk? How much is it?* *Many* for things you count, *much* for things you do not, and for prices.

**Writing**

- `writing-what-people-are-wearing` Writing What People Are Wearing (judged by rules; after `present-continuous`, `writing-capitals-and-periods`). Turn notes into present continuous sentences: *Mia / a blue dress* becomes *Mia is wearing a blue dress.*

**Conversation:** `buying-clothes`. The tutor works in a clothes shop. Task: Ask for a piece of clothing in your size and a color you like, ask the price, and decide whether to buy it.

**Later, with audio and recording:** Listening to prices and choosing the right amount; describing what someone is wearing.

**Vocabulary**

- `a1-words-school-subjects` School Subjects (12): math, science (sciences), art (arts), history, class (classes), lesson (lessons), subject (subjects), test (tests), learn (learns, learned, learning), teach (teaches, taught, teaching), easy (easier, easiest), difficult

### 9. The Library

**Vocabulary**

- `a1-words-library` The Library (12): library (libraries), borrow (borrows, borrowed, borrowing), return (returns, returned, returning), shelf (shelves), card (cards), due, story (stories), librarian (librarians), quiet (quieter, quietest), find (finds, found, finding), need (needs, needed, needing), back

**Grammar**

- `object-pronouns` Me, Him, Her and Them (after `my-your-his-her`, `a1-words-books`). *Can you help me? I like her. Give them to us.* After a verb or a word like *to* and *with*, *I* becomes *me*, *he* becomes *him*, *they* becomes *them*.

**Conversation:** `library-help-desk`. The tutor is the librarian at the help desk of a school library (an existing conversation). Task: Say what your project is about, ask for a book on it, and find out when it is due back.

**Later, with audio and recording:** Listening to a librarian's directions and dates; asking *Can I borrow...?* and *When is it due?*

### 12. Town and Travel

**Vocabulary**

- `a1-words-town` Town (12): town (towns), street (streets), road (roads), bus (buses), car (cars), train (trains), station (stations), bank (banks), restaurant (restaurants), hotel (hotels), museum (museums), map (maps)
- `a1-words-directions` Directions (12): left, turn (turns, turned, turning), straight, stop (stops, stopped, stopping), cross (crosses, crossed, crossing), corner (corners), far (farther, farthest), way (ways), ticket (tickets), travel (travels, traveled, traveling), visit (visits, visited, visiting), plane (planes)

**Grammar**

- `wh-questions` Where, When and What Questions (after `present-simple-questions`, `questions-with-be`). *Where do you live? When does the train leave?* The question word comes first, then *do* or *does*; Chinese leaves the question word in its place (*nǐ zhù zài nǎr?*).
- `can-for-requests` Can I...? Can You...? (after `can-for-ability`, `a1-words-directions`). *Can I have a ticket, please? Can you tell me the way?* *Can* also asks for things and for help.

- `writing-directions-to-your-home` Writing Directions (judged by a model; after `imperatives`, `prepositions-of-place`, `a1-words-directions`, `writing-a-short-note`). Write a message of four to six sentences that tells a friend how to get from a place the task names to your home.

**Conversation:** `asking-the-way`. The tutor is a person in the street of a town you are visiting. Task: Ask the way to the station, check the directions, and thank them.

**Later, with audio and recording:** Listening to directions and following them on a map; asking for and giving directions.
