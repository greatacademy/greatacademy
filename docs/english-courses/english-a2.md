# English A2

> This is Great Academy's own outline. Its design is in the [English curriculum](../english-curriculum.md) §6.2. [简体中文](english-a2.zh-CN.md).

- **Course:** `english-a2`
- **Level:** CEFR A2, for learners of English as a foreign language
- **Prerequisite(s):** A1, or the present simple and continuous, *was* and *were*, and about 600 words
- **Language of explanation:** The interface language: Chinese or English
- **Size:** 12 units · 88 topics (51 of vocabulary, 22 of grammar, 15 of writing) · 592 headwords, 1227 by the end of the level

## Aim

By the end of A2 a learner understands sentences and frequent expressions about areas of immediate relevance, such as family, shopping, the local area and work, and can describe in simple terms their background, their surroundings and past events (CEFR A2). They know about 1,200 words, read texts of 100 to 220 words, and write short emails, stories, descriptions and messages.

The grammar adds the past and the future: the past simple of regular and irregular verbs with its questions and negatives, the past continuous, *going to* and *will*, the present perfect with *ever*, *never*, *just*, *already* and *yet*; then comparisons, adverbs, *should*, *must* and *have to*, possessives and quantities. Writing moves from sentences to short texts judged by a model.

## How to Read This Outline

Each unit lists its topics by strand. A vocabulary topic teaches its batch of headwords; the spellings in parentheses are the forms a passage of this level or a higher one may also use. Every word is taught once in all levels, and a passage uses only words taught at its level or below. Grammar and writing topics list their prerequisites; a topic marked *existing* is already in the graph and keeps its own prerequisites. Writing topics are judged by rules or by a model (§8). The reading unit and the conversation are written from this outline in a later step; listening and speaking wait for audio and recording, and each unit says what it will gain.

**Grammar**

- `past-simple-regular` The Past Simple: Regular Verbs (after `was-and-were`, `present-simple`). *We played. She visited.* Regular verbs add *-ed* for every person; Chinese marks the past with *le* or a time word, not on the verb.
- `past-simple-irregular` The Past Simple: Irregular Verbs (after `past-simple-regular`). *go, went; see, saw; buy, bought.* The commonest verbs have past forms of their own, learned one by one.
- `past-simple-questions-and-negatives` Did and Didn't (after `past-simple-irregular`, `present-simple-questions`). *Did you go? I didn't see it.* After *did* and *didn't* the verb goes back to its plain form: not *Did you went?*

**Writing**

- `writing-sentences-in-the-past` Writing Sentences in the Past (judged by rules; after `past-simple-irregular`, `writing-sentences-about-routines`). Turn notes about last weekend into past simple sentences: *Sam / go / the zoo / Saturday* becomes *Sam went to the zoo on Saturday.*
- `writing-about-your-weekend` Writing About Your Weekend (judged by a model; after `writing-sentences-in-the-past`, `writing-a-short-note`). Write five to seven sentences about what you did last weekend, in the past simple, in the order it happened.

**Reading:** `a2-reading-last-weekend`, 8 passages: A picnic that went wrong; Camping by the lake; A visit to Grandma's village; The lost tent; A rainy Saturday at the gallery; Cousins at the beach; The day I forgot my keys; A surprise for Dad.

**Conversation:** `telling-about-your-weekend`. The tutor is a classmate on Monday morning. Task: Tell what you did last weekend, ask what they did, and react to their news.

**Later, with audio and recording:** Listening to a story of a weekend and putting events in order; telling your weekend aloud in the past simple, with the right *-ed* sounds.

**Conversation:** `at-the-doctor`. The tutor is a doctor at a clinic. Task: Say what is wrong and since when, answer the doctor's questions, and check what you should do.

- `verbs-followed-by-to` Want To, Decide To (after `present-simple`, `a2-words-events`). *I want to go. She decided to stay. We hope to see you.* Some verbs take *to* and a verb after them.
- `subordinating-conjunctions` Subordinating Conjunctions (after `coordinating-conjunctions`, *existing*). *because, when, before, after, if*: a part of a sentence that cannot stand alone.

**Writing**

- `writing-an-invitation-and-a-reply` Writing an Invitation and a Reply (judged by a model; after `writing-a-short-note`, `will-for-the-future`). Write an invitation that says what, when and where, or a reply that accepts or says no with a reason.

**Reading:** `a2-reading-festivals`, 8 passages: The Lantern Festival; A wedding in the village; Fireworks at midnight; A surprise party that wasn't a surprise; Costumes for the parade; Mooncakes and the moon; Birthdays around the world; The invitation that got lost.

**Conversation:** `planning-a-party`. The tutor is a friend planning a party with you. Task: Agree on the day, the food and the music, and decide who will invite the guests.

**Later, with audio and recording:** Listening to people's plans for a festival and matching them; giving and answering an invitation aloud.

### 12. Jobs and Dreams

**Vocabulary**

- `a2-words-jobs-2` More Jobs (12): engineer (engineers), scientist (scientists), artist (artists), singer (singers), actor (actors), pilot (pilots), waiter (waiters), chef (chefs), writer (writers), mechanic (mechanics), firefighter (firefighters), businessman (businessmen)

- `a2-words-ambitions` Ambitions (12): become (becomes, became, becoming), succeed (succeeds, succeeded, succeeding), success (successes), fail (fails, failed, failing), improve (improves, improved, improving), study (studies, studied, studying), university (universities), college (colleges), graduate (graduates, graduated, graduating), course (courses), ambition (ambitions), hard-working

**Grammar**

- `subject-and-object-questions` Who Called You? Who Did You Call? (after `wh-questions`, `past-simple-questions-and-negatives`). *Who called you?* asks about the one who did it and has no *did*; *Who did you call?* asks about the other one.

**Writing**

- `writing-about-your-dream-job` Writing About a Dream Job (judged by a model; after `writing-about-future-plans`, `expanding-with-because-but-so`). Write six to eight sentences about a job you would like: what it is, why you want it, and what you will do to get it.

**Reading:** `a2-reading-jobs`, 8 passages: A pilot's long day; The chef who was a scientist; My first interview; From waiter to manager; Firefighters at work; A dream that came true; The mechanic's daughter; Jobs that robots can't do.

**Conversation:** `a-summer-job-interview`. The tutor is the manager of a cafe that needs summer staff. Task: Answer questions about yourself, say what you can do and when you are free, and ask one question about the job.

**Later, with audio and recording:** Listening to people describe their jobs and noting the details; answering interview questions aloud.
