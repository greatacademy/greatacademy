# English, Grades 3 to 5

> This is Great Academy's own outline. Its design is in the [English curriculum](../english-curriculum.md) §11. [简体中文](english-grades-3-5.zh-CN.md).

- **Course:** `english-grades-3-5`
- **Level:** Grades 3 to 5, for learners whose first language is English or who read English as well as a native speaker of their age
- **Prerequisite(s):** Reading simple stories and writing simple sentences
- **Language of explanation:** English
- **Size:** 9 units · 78 topics (56 existing, 22 new: 10 of vocabulary, 7 of grammar and mechanics, 5 of writing) · 9 reading units of 88 passages · 9 conversations

## Aim

By the end of grade 5 a learner reads informational texts and stories of the grade band on their own, finds their main ideas and how they are organized, and draws inferences from them. They write complete, correctly punctuated sentences with the verb tenses and conjunctions of the band, and paragraphs of several kinds: a story, a summary, an opinion, instructions, a comparison, causes and effects, and a description. They know the words of nine themes from science, history and stories, and how prefixes, suffixes and roots build words.

## How to Read This Outline

Every unit is a theme and teaches four things together: the words of the theme, grammar and punctuation, a reading skill, and writing. It ends with a reading unit on the theme and a conversation. Topics marked *existing* are in the graph already and keep their own prerequisites; each new topic lists its prerequisites. Every existing topic of grades 3 to 5 is in exactly one unit.

Depth: each new grammar knowledge point has 30 to 48 items built from hand-written cores by templates; each word of a vocabulary batch appears in three sentences, so each vocabulary knowledge point has 30 items; each judged writing knowledge point has 12 tasks with a model; reading units have 8 to 10 passages of 140 to 260 words, with three questions each.

Listening and speaking wait for audio and recording: until then a unit lists its conversation but does not offer it, and each unit says what it will gain.

## Units

### 1. Stories and Fables

**Words:** `context-clues` *(existing)*; `words-for-character-traits` Words for Character Traits (after `context-clues`): generous, stubborn, timid, boastful, loyal, curious, cautious, greedy, humble, determined.

**Grammar:** `nouns-and-pronouns`, `subjects-and-predicates`, `complete-sentences-and-fragments` *(existing)*.

**Reading:** `making-inferences`, `characters`, `point-of-view`, `lesson-of-a-story` *(existing)*.

**Writing:** `capitals-and-end-punctuation`, `punctuating-dialogue` *(existing)*.

**Reading unit:** `stories-and-fables` *(existing)*, 10 passages: The Missing Lunch; The Dragon Notebook; The Spelling Bee; The Cardboard Castle; The Kite Under the Stairs; Two Sides of the Fence; The Mole Who Dug Too Fast; The Sunflower and the Clover; Snow Day; The Class Play.

**Conversation:** `fable-for-a-younger-cousin`. The tutor is a sleepy younger cousin at bedtime. Task: tell a fable with animal characters from beginning to end and say its lesson.

**Later, with audio and recording:** Listening to a fable read aloud and retelling its events in order; reading dialogue aloud with expression.

### 2. Water on Earth

**Words:** `words-of-the-water-cycle` Words of the Water Cycle (after `context-clues`): reservoir, droplet, moisture, absorb, seep, irrigate, conserve, drought, runoff, condense; `academic-words-1` *(existing)*.

**Grammar:** `plural-nouns` Plural Nouns (after `nouns-and-pronouns`): spelling regular plurals with *-s*, *-es*, *y* to *i* and *f* to *v*, and irregular plurals such as *mice*, *teeth* and *sheep*. `simple-verb-tenses`, `subject-verb-agreement`, `coordinating-conjunctions` *(existing)*.

**Reading:** `main-idea`, `supporting-details` *(existing)*.

**Writing:** `commas-in-a-series`, `expanding-with-because-but-so` *(existing)*.

**Reading unit:** `water-on-earth` *(existing)*, 10 passages: Where Earth's Water Is; Three Forms of Water; Where Puddles Go; How Clouds Form; Rain, Snow and Hail; A Journey Without an End; The Life of a River; Water Under Our Feet; Rivers of Ice; Every Drop Counts.

**Conversation:** `water-saving-family-plan`. The tutor is the learner's parent after a high water bill. Task: talk about where the family uses water and agree on three ways to save it.

**Later, with audio and recording:** Listening to a short radio report on a drought and noting its main idea; explaining the water cycle aloud in order.

**Reading:** `sequence-in-texts` *(existing)*.

**Writing:** `expanding-ideas-in-your-own-words` *(existing)*.

**Later, with audio and recording:** Listening to instructions for planting seeds and putting the steps in order; giving instructions aloud.

### 9. The Solar System

**Words:** `words-of-the-solar-system` Words of the Solar System (after `context-clues`): orbit, axis, rotate, galaxy, comet, asteroid, telescope, astronaut, gravity, constellation. `multiple-meaning-words` Words with More Than One Meaning (after `context-clues`): twelve words with three meanings each, such as *bark*, *bank*, *scale* and *current*.

**Grammar:** `correlative-conjunctions` Paired Conjunctions (after `coordinating-conjunctions`, `subject-verb-agreement`): *either … or*, *neither … nor*, *both … and*, *not only … but also*, and the verb that agrees with the subjects they join.

**Reading:** `similes-and-metaphors`, `idioms` *(existing)*.

**Writing:** `writing-a-narrative-paragraph` Writing a Story in a Paragraph (judged by a model; after `writing-a-paragraph`, `punctuating-dialogue`, `verb-tense-consistency`). `writing-a-descriptive-paragraph` Writing a Descriptive Paragraph (judged by a model; after `similes-and-metaphors`, `order-of-adjectives`, `writing-a-paragraph`).

**Reading unit:** `our-solar-system` *(new)*, 8 passages: Our Star, the Sun; Why We Have Day and Night; The Changing Moon; The Rocky Planets; The Giant Planets; Pictures in the Stars; Comets and Asteroids; Exploring Space.

**Conversation:** `planetarium-report-questions`. The tutor presents a planetarium show. Task: choose one planet and find out three facts about it for a report.

**Later, with audio and recording:** Listening to a planetarium show and noting three facts; describing the night sky aloud with a simile.
