# English, Grade 8

> This is Great Academy's own outline. Its design is in the [English curriculum](../english-curriculum.md) §6.3 and the [development plan](../english-development-plan.md) §E8. [简体中文](english-grade-8.zh-CN.md).

- **Course:** `english-grade-8`
- **Level:** Grade 8, for learners whose first language is English or who read English as well as a native speaker of their age
- **Prerequisite(s):** `english-grade-7`, or reading and writing arguments, reports, stories and speeches at the level of grade 7
- **Language of explanation:** English
- **Size:** 10 units · 94 topics (15 existing, 79 new: 35 of vocabulary, 12 of grammar and mechanics, 18 of reading, 7 of writing checked by rules, 7 of writing judged by a model) · about 1,420 XP · 9 reading units of 72 passages (3 existing, 6 new) · 10 conversations (3 existing, 7 new)

## Aim

By the end of grade 8 a learner reads the histories, founding documents, science texts, plays, Gothic tales and oral histories of the grade on their own and judges how well they are argued and told. In texts of facts they track a central idea, tell facts from interpretations and judgments, weigh a scientific argument and set aside irrelevant evidence, read a founding document, acknowledge conflicting viewpoints and compare conflicting accounts of one event, a memoir with a report, and several texts on one topic; they see how a paragraph is built and evaluate an argument as a whole. In literature they follow irony and dramatic irony, foreshadowing, flashback and symbolism, see how incidents propel the action, doubt an unreliable narrator, recognize the elements of the Gothic, read a scene of Shakespeare and compare a play with a production of it, and see how modern stories use old tales. They use verbals, gerund and infinitive phrases in parallel, simple, compound and complex sentences with their commas and the right subordinating conjunction, the subjunctive in wishes and unreal conditions, voice without shifting it, who and whom, prepositions before relative pronouns, hyphens in compound modifiers, dashes, parentheses and ellipses, and they read inverted sentences. They summarize a technical text, quote a primary source and a person they have interviewed, use statistics in a sentence, qualify a claim, create atmosphere with details of the senses and write stage directions; and they write an argument about history, an informative report from sources, a scientific explanation, a Gothic tale, a scene for the stage, a reflective essay and an essay on a retold tale. They know the words of ten themes: turning points in stories, the machines that changed the world, voyages of exploration, the age of revolutions, the industrial age, the story of life, tales of dread, Shakespeare's stage, migration, and right and wrong.

## How to Read This Outline

Every unit is a theme and teaches four things together: the words of the theme, grammar and mechanics, reading skills, and writing. It ends with a reading unit on the theme and a conversation, except the last, which has a conversation but no reading unit. Topics marked *existing* are in the graph already, in the band course `english-grades-6-8`, and keep their ids and their own prerequisites; each new topic lists its prerequisites, which may lie in an earlier unit or an earlier grade. A new topic of words lists the words it teaches, none of which another batch of the native courses teaches; any other new topic says what it teaches and its knowledge points as steps. A judged writing topic names its task and the criteria of its rubric, marking the criteria that are new.

Strands: of the 94 topics, 38 teach words (40%), 14 grammar and mechanics (15%), 22 reading (23%), 8 writing checked by rules (9%) and 12 writing judged by a model (13%).

Depth: each new grammar, reading or rule-checked writing knowledge point has 30 to 48 items built from hand-written cores by templates; each word of a vocabulary batch appears in three sentences, so each vocabulary knowledge point has 30 items; each judged writing knowledge point has 10 tasks with a model; new reading units have 8 passages of 200 to 400 words, with three questions each. A batch of ten words earns about 15 XP, a word-study, grammar, reading or rule-checked writing topic about 14 and a judged writing topic about 24, and the existing topics keep their own XP, so the lessons of the grade come to about 1,420 XP.

Listening and speaking wait for audio and recording: until then a unit lists its conversation but does not offer it, and each unit says what it will gain.

The band course `english-grades-6-8` retires once `english-grade-6`, `english-grade-7` and `english-grade-8` hold all of its topics, which they now do. Its 55 topics are divided among the three grades by its units, each in exactly one, with its id unchanged: 24 in grade 6 (myths, poetry and Rome), 16 in grade 7 (argument and oceans) and 15 in grade 8 (turning points, machines and exploration). Its reading units and conversations go with their units.

## What the Next Grade Builds On

`english-grade-9` builds on this year: the argument about history and the essay on a retold tale grow into the analytical essay and the argument with sources; tracking a central idea and evaluating an argument go on to rhetoric, a speaker's purpose and the development of a theme; the reading of Shakespeare goes on to a whole tragedy; and grammar moves to conditional sentences, sentence variety for effect and the punctuation of the essay.
