# IELTS Academic

> This is Great Academy's own outline. Its design is in the [English curriculum](../english-curriculum.md) §6.2. [简体中文](english-ielts.zh-CN.md).

- **Course:** `english-ielts`, in the group English Exams
- **Level:** CEFR B2 and above, for learners of English as a foreign language preparing for IELTS Academic
- **Prerequisite(s):** English B2 (`english-b2`)
- **Language of explanation:** The interface language: Chinese or English
- **Size:** 3 units · 15 topics (5 new, all practice-only) · 1 reading unit of 6 passages

## The Test

Checked on 2026-11-10 against the official descriptions at [ielts.org: Academic Reading](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/test-types/ielts-academic-test/ielts-academic-format-reading) and [ielts.org: Academic Writing](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/test-types/ielts-academic-test/ielts-academic-format-writing).

| Paper | Format | In this course |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Reading | 60 minutes, 3 texts of 2,150 to 2,750 words in all, 40 questions; types include multiple choice, True/False/Not given, Yes/No/Not given, matching information, matching headings, matching features, matching sentence endings, sentence, summary, note, table and flow-chart completion, diagram labels and short answers | Unit 1 |
| Writing | 60 minutes; Task 1: at least 150 words describing a graph, table, chart or diagram (about 20 minutes); Task 2: at least 250 words answering a point of view, argument or problem (about 40 minutes, counting twice as much). Judged on task achievement or response, coherence and cohesion, lexical resource, and grammatical range and accuracy | Units 2 and 3 |
| Listening, Speaking | 30 minutes of recordings; a face-to-face interview of 11 to 14 minutes | Not yet: see below |

Scores are overall bands from 0 to 9 in half bands. | Overall band | 4.0 to 5.0 | 5.5 to 6.5 | 7.0 to 8.0 | 8.5 to 9.0 |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| CEFR | B1 | B2 | C1 | C2 |

Finishing English B2 aims at about 5.5 to 6.5; this course aims at 6.5 and above, toward the 7 that C1 and many universities ask for.

Questions, texts and word lists of the test are never copied: every text and item here is written for this course in the formats above.

## Aim

The course turns the reading and writing of B2 into the habits IELTS rewards: telling what a text states from what it does not, finding the paragraph that says something, describing data precisely, and arguing an essay in a fixed time. It teaches nothing new. Its own topics are practice-only: a lesson starts with practice, and the explanation appears only after a failed attempt. The topics it reuses keep their place in the graph, so a learner who has learned them at B2 or in the native courses is credited and moves on.

## How to Read This Outline

Each unit lists the topics it reuses (*existing*) and its new topics. A new topic has two knowledge points of rising difficulty, as the exam topics of mathematics do, and at least 30 items each when judged by rules, or 12 tasks with model answers when judged by a model. Passages use only words of B2 and below, apart from names and terms they explain; they are shorter than the test's (about 400 to 500 words rather than 700 to 900), so that each can be read in one sitting.

## Units

### 1. Academic Reading

**Existing:** `main-idea`, `supporting-details`, `making-inferences`, `fact-and-opinion`, `claims`, `reading-charts-and-statistics`.

**New**

- `ielts-true-false-not-given` True, False or Not Given (practice-only; after `fact-and-opinion`, `making-inferences`). Statements about short texts: (1) *True / False / Not given* about facts; (2) *Yes / No / Not given* about a writer's views and claims.
- `ielts-matching-headings` Matching Headings and Information (practice-only; after `supporting-details`). Texts of four lettered paragraphs: (1) choose the heading of a paragraph; (2) find the paragraph that contains a piece of information.

**Reading:** `ielts-reading`, 6 passages of 400 to 500 words in IELTS question formats: A short history of glass; How honeybees choose a home; Why we sleep; Cities that make their own weather; The last speakers; Paying for museums.

**Later, with audio and recording:** Listening sections 1 to 4 (forms, maps, discussions and a lecture); the three parts of the Speaking interview.

### 2. Writing Task 1

**Existing:** `b2-words-trends`, `comparatives-and-superlatives`, `writing-a-report`.

**New**

- `ielts-describing-data` Describing Data (practice-only; after `b2-words-trends`, `comparatives-and-superlatives`). Choose the sentence that reports a table accurately: (1) trends between two years, sharp, slight or stable; (2) proportions and comparisons, such as *about a quarter* or *roughly twice as high*.
- `ielts-writing-task-1` Writing Task 1 (judged by a model; practice-only; after `ielts-describing-data`, `writing-a-report`). Describe a table or chart given as figures in at least 150 words: an overview, the main features, and comparisons with figures.

### 3. Writing Task 2

**Existing:** `writing-a-thesis-statement`, `introductions-and-conclusions`, `writing-an-opinion-essay`, `hedging-and-qualification`, `b2-words-linking-1`.

**New**

- `ielts-writing-task-2` Writing Task 2 (judged by a model; practice-only; after `writing-an-opinion-essay`). An essay of at least 250 words on a view, a debate or a problem: a clear position throughout, developed and supported ideas, paragraphs that link, and every part of the question answered.
