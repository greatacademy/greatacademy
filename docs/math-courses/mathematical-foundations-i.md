## Description

Our Mathematical Foundations I course is the starting point of a three-course sequence designed to take ambitious adult learners from fractions to calculus, preparing them for university-level mathematics.

Beginning with essential arithmetic, the course develops the numerical fluency, algebraic reasoning, and problem-solving skills needed to make rapid progress toward higher mathematics. Students move from fractions, ratios, and percentages into equations, functions, exponential relationships, polynomials, and geometry, building a strong mathematical foundation without unnecessary detours.

Designed for adults returning to mathematics or addressing gaps in their foundational knowledge, this course provides a systematic and efficient route into more advanced study. By developing both computational fluency and conceptual understanding, students gain the tools to manipulate expressions, solve equations, interpret graphs, model relationships, and reason geometrically. Upon completing this course, students will be prepared to continue into Mathematical Foundations II, progressing toward calculus and university-level mathematics.

## Overview

The course covers fractions, developing a thorough understanding of fraction arithmetic through visual models and symbolic methods. Students add and subtract fractions and whole numbers, find least common multiples and denominators, compare fractions, calculate with unlike denominators, add and subtract mixed numbers, multiply fractions and mixed numbers, and divide fractions using reciprocals and equivalent representations.

The study of ratios and percentages includes simplifying and comparing ratios, using ratio tables and graphs, calculating unit rates, solving proportional problems, and modeling direct and inverse variation. Students also work with units of measurement, perform conversions, interpret units in formulas, account for measurement accuracy, convert among fractions, decimals, and percentages, and solve problems involving percentage increases, decreases, and successive changes.

Working with the number system extends students' arithmetic skills to positive and negative rational numbers. They calculate with signed integers, fractions, and decimals; use additive inverses and absolute value; apply divisibility rules; work with prime factorization, greatest common factors, and least common multiples; classify number types; and convert between fractions and terminating or repeating decimals.

The course covers exponents and radicals. Students evaluate positive, zero, and negative exponents; apply the rules of exponents; use order of operations; calculate square roots and cube roots; simplify radical expressions; rationalize denominators; and connect radicals with fractional exponents.

In equations and inequalities, students construct and evaluate algebraic expressions, identify terms and coefficients, collect like terms, apply the distributive law, factor linear expressions, solve one-step and multi-step linear equations, rearrange formulas, and model real-world situations algebraically. They also solve linear and compound inequalities, use interval notation, and solve equations using square and higher-order roots.

The course includes two-variable equations. Students develop fluency with the Cartesian coordinate system, graph linear equations, calculate slopes, work with slope-intercept, point-slope, and standard forms, and analyze parallel lines. They construct linear models, interpret distance-time and speed-time graphs, solve systems of equations by substitution and elimination, and analyze systems with one solution, no solution, or infinitely many solutions.

Work with radical and rational expressions involves simplifying algebraic radicals, applying radical rules, simplifying rational expressions by factoring, and solving rational equations. The study of functions includes developing the concept of a function, interpreting its domain and range, identifying roots and extrema, analyzing increasing and decreasing behavior, evaluating piecewise functions, and constructing linear functions from mathematical and real-world information.

The course covers absolute value and exponential functions. Students simplify absolute value expressions, solve absolute value equations and inequalities, and graph transformed absolute value functions. They apply exponent rules to algebraic expressions, solve exponential equations, construct exponential growth and decay models, interpret their parameters, and graph exponential functions.

Polynomial topics include identifying polynomial terms, degrees, and coefficients; simplifying, adding, subtracting, and multiplying polynomials; using special algebraic identities; and factoring polynomials using greatest common factors, differences of squares, perfect square trinomials, and general trinomial factoring techniques.

Geometry topics include points, lines, segments, angles, parallel and perpendicular relationships, midpoints, perpendicular bisectors, and basic circle geometry. Students also analyze triangles and polygons, apply the Pythagorean theorem and special right-triangle relationships, use interior and exterior angle formulas, study quadrilateral properties, and calculate perimeters and areas of plane figures.
