## Description

Our SAT Math Fundamentals course develops the complete mathematical foundation required to excel on the math section of the SAT exam, spanning every tested topic from Prealgebra through Algebra II and Trigonometry.

In addition to core school-curriculum topics, this course explicitly teaches over 120 critical "missing middle" topics - specialized problem structures, multi-step syntheses, and non-routine reasoning skills that appear regularly on the SAT, but are rarely taught in standard high-school courses. Because the SAT gives students only about 90 seconds per question on average, encountering these problem types for the first time on exam day can derail even top students; mastering them in advance through explicit, scaffolded instruction is essential for achieving a top score.

Filling in foundational and "missing middle" gaps is the first and most important step toward preparing for the exam. Once students complete SAT Math Fundamentals, they are prepared to advance into our SAT Math Prep course, as well as follow the structured test preparation process described in our guide on how to prepare for a standardized math test .

## Overview

This course covers the full breadth of skills represented on the SAT Math exam - combining the core school curriculum (Prealgebra, Algebra I, Geometry, and Algebra II) with the "missing middle" skills that bridge the gap between textbook exercises and actual exam problems.

Students begin by taking a diagnostic assessment, which will identify their current knowledge frontier and any weaknesses in their foundational knowledge.

Once the student has completed the diagnostic assessment, our sophisticated algorithms will create a custom course that repairs any upstream prerequisite weaknesses first, then systematically builds fluency across both the core curriculum and the specialized problem structures tested on the SAT.

Foundational topics include rational-number arithmetic, exponents, radicals, order of operations, algebraic expressions, and simplifying linear expressions. These skills support the numerical and symbolic fluency needed across the full range of SAT math problems, including manipulating and comparing radical and fractional-exponent expressions.

The course covers linear equations and inequalities. Students solve and model with one-variable and two-variable linear equations, interpret coefficients in context, analyze lines in slope-intercept, point-slope, and standard form, solve systems of linear equations and inequalities, and optimize sums, differences, products, and quotients under constraints. Special emphasis is placed on solving systems by identifying algebraic structure, parametrizing systems with infinitely many solutions, and determining unknown parameters that produce a specified number of solutions.

The geometry and trigonometry portion of the course develops students' understanding of points, lines, angles, triangles, polygons, solids, congruence, similarity, length, area, volume, circles, radians, and right-triangle trigonometry. In addition to standard geometric theorems, students learn to reason from verbal descriptions of congruent and similar figures, analyze inscribed polygons and circles, construct functions representing properties of 2D shapes and 3D solids, work with composite solids and combined prisms, and evaluate trigonometric ratios of large angles in degrees and radians.

Advanced algebra topics include functions, tabular representations of linear functions, graph transformations, absolute value expressions and functions, polynomials, quadratic equations and functions, nonlinear systems, exponential equations and models, rational expressions and equations, and radical equations. Students develop fluency with high-leverage SAT structures such as factoring expressions with hidden factors, equating polynomial coefficients, reasoning about quadratic coefficients using symmetry and the factor theorem, determining unknown parameters in quadratic and linear-quadratic systems, and time-shifting or rewriting exponential models into equivalent forms.

Problem-solving, ratios, percentages, units, statistics, and probability are also covered. Students solve multi-stage commerce and finance percentage problems, manipulate ratios, convert compound and squared units, and interpret rates of change in physical phenomena, while developing statistical and probabilistic reasoning across sampling, margin of error, frequency tables, histograms, dot plots, box plots, effects of data transformations on measures of center and spread, scatter plots, regression models, and conditional probability.

## Outcomes

Upon successful completion of this course, students will have mastered the following: Preliminaries

- Adding, subtracting, multiplying, and dividing rational numbers, and solving real-world and mathematical problems involving these operations.
- Manipulating expressions involving exponents and radicals, fractional and negative exponents, and the rules of exponents and radicals.
- Simplifying, comparing, adding, subtracting, and rationalizing radical expressions, including denominators with two terms.
- Applying the order of operations to evaluate complex expressions containing rational numbers, fractional and negative exponents, and radicals.
- Constructing, evaluating, simplifying, and factoring linear algebraic expressions.
- Solving one-step and multi-step linear equations, including cases involving binomials, rational expressions, cross-multiplication, unknown coefficients, and multiple variables.
- Manipulating expressions and rearranging multi-variable equations and formulas.
- Identifying linear equations with one solution, no solution, or infinitely many solutions.
- Using linear equations to model and solve real-world and mathematical problems, including consecutive integer, speed-distance-time, work, and mixture problems.
- Graphing linear equations and calculating and describing a graph's key features.
- Working with slope-intercept, point-slope, and standard forms of straight-line equations and determining properties of lines presented in these forms.
- Finding equations of parallel and perpendicular lines.
- Using two-variable linear equations to model totals, interpret coefficients in context, and analyze distance-time and speed-time graphs.
- Solving two-variable systems of linear equations using substitution, elimination, and algebraic structure, including larger systems and cases with fractional and decimal coefficients.
- Identifying and parametrizing systems with no solutions or infinitely many solutions, and determining unknown parameters in systems of linear equations.
- Using two-variable systems to model and solve contextual problems.
- Solving single-variable linear inequalities, compound inequalities, and inequalities with unknown parameters, and graphing their solutions on number lines.
- Using interval notation, including unbounded intervals, unions, and intersections.
- Optimizing sums, differences, products, and quotients under inequality constraints.
- Solving two-variable linear inequalities and systems of inequalities using graphical methods.
- Modeling real-world constraints using single-variable, compound, and two-variable systems of linear inequalities.
- Understanding and applying foundational geometry concepts, including working with segments, midpoints, and angle relationships.
- Solving problems involving complementary, non-adjacent complementary, supplementary, vertical, corresponding, alternate, and consecutive angles, as well as parallel line criteria.
- Solving problems with interior and exterior angles of triangles and polygons.

- Applying the Pythagorean theorem and properties of 45-45-90, 30-60-90, and equilateral triangles.

- Applying the midpoint and triangle proportionality theorems.
- Solving problems related to polygon and solid similarity, including area and volume scale factors.
- Calculating perimeters and areas of polygons and composite shapes in the plane, including border problems.
- Applying the distance and midpoint formulas in the coordinate plane.
- Analyzing equilateral triangles and other polygons inscribed in circles.
- Solving problems related to the surface area, volume, and diagonals of three-dimensional figures, including cubes, rectangular solids, pyramids, cylinders, cones, spheres, composite solids, and combined prisms.
- Constructing functions that represent properties, areas, and volumes of two-dimensional shapes and three-dimensional solids.
- Understanding circle fundamentals, including interiors, exteriors, central and inscribed angles, arcs, sectors, segments, Thales' theorem, and inscribed quadrilaterals.
- Calculating circumferences, arc lengths, and areas of circles and circular sectors in both degrees and radians.
- Solving problems involving tangent lines, chords, and isosceles triangles in circles.
- Working with equations of circles in the coordinate plane, completing the square to find center and radius, finding intersections of circles and lines, and transforming circles.
- Using right-triangle trigonometric ratios and reciprocal ratios to calculate side lengths and angle measures, including from verbal descriptions.
- Extending trigonometric ratios using the unit circle, reference angles, and cofunction identities.
- Evaluating trigonometric ratios of special, quadrantal, negative, and large angles in both degrees and radians.
- Modeling and solving real-world problems using right-triangle trigonometry.
- Understanding function representations, domain and range, the vertical line test, global and local extrema, roots, end behavior, increasing and decreasing intervals, average rate of change, piecewise functions, function composition, and inverse functions.

- Transforming functions using translations, stretches, and reflections, including transforming polynomial and reciprocal functions.
- Applying the rules of absolute value and solving absolute value equations.
- Graphing and transforming absolute value functions, including translations, stretches, reflections, and composite transformations.
- Determining domains, ranges, and roots of transformed absolute value functions.

- Equating polynomial coefficients and applying the factor and remainder theorems.
- Solving quadratic equations by factoring, the square root method, completing the square, and the quadratic formula.
- Using the discriminant and Vieta's formulas to determine unknown parameters in quadratic equations with zero, one, or two real solutions.
- Graphing and transforming quadratic functions, using vertex form and symmetry to reason about coefficients, and fitting quadratic functions to data.
- Solving and modeling with linear-quadratic and nonlinear systems of equations, including determining unknown parameters for a given number of intersection points.
- Graphing higher-order polynomial functions and identifying valid outputs of factored polynomials.
- Rewriting radical and fractional-exponent expressions and applying the laws of exponents to algebraic expressions.
- Solving exponential equations and modeling exponential growth, decay, and compound interest in context.
- Working with equivalent forms of exponential functions, time-shifting exponential models, and determining unknown parameters.
- Analyzing the graphs, transformations, domains, ranges, intercepts, asymptotes, and extrema of exponential functions.
- Simplifying, adding, subtracting, multiplying, and dividing rational expressions, including fractions of fractions and polynomial factorization.
- Solving rational equations, including many-variable rational and nonlinear equations.
- Simplifying algebraic radical expressions and solving radical equations.
- Solving multi-stage problems involving ratios, equivalent ratios, unit rates, direct and inverse variation, and multipart commerce and finance percentages.
- Determining and interpreting units and rates of change in formulas and physical phenomena, including density.
- Performing multi-step unit conversions using algebraic techniques, including mixed and squared units of area.

- Interpreting scatter plots, trend lines, residuals, correlation coefficients, and linear, quadratic, and exponential regression models.
- Calculating probabilities of single and compound events using sample spaces, Venn diagrams, two-way frequency tables, conditional probability, and independence.
