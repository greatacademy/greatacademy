## Description

Our ACT Math Fundamentals course develops the complete mathematical foundation required to excel on the math section of the ACT exam, spanning every tested topic from Prealgebra through Precalculus.

In addition to core school-curriculum topics, this course explicitly teaches over 150 critical "missing middle" topics - specialized problem structures, multi-step syntheses, and non-routine reasoning skills that appear regularly on the ACT, but are rarely taught in standard high-school courses. Because the ACT gives students only about a minute per question on average, encountering these problem types for the first time on exam day can derail even top students; mastering them in advance through explicit, scaffolded instruction is essential for achieving a top score.

Filling in foundational and "missing middle" gaps is the first and most important step toward preparing for the exam. To achieve the best possible outcome, students should pair this course with a structured and intentional test preparation process, which we describe in our guide on how to prepare for a standardized math test .

## Overview

This course covers the full breadth of skills represented on the ACT Math exam - combining the core high-school curriculum (Prealgebra, Algebra I, Geometry, Algebra II, and Precalculus) with the "missing middle" skills that bridge the gap between textbook exercises and actual exam problems.

Students begin by taking a diagnostic assessment, which will identify their current knowledge frontier and any weaknesses in their foundational knowledge.

Once the student has completed the diagnostic assessment, our sophisticated algorithms will create a custom course that repairs any upstream prerequisite weaknesses first, then systematically builds fluency across both the advanced curriculum and the specialized problem structures tested on the ACT.

Foundational topics include rational-number arithmetic, exponents, scientific notation, radicals, order of operations, number theory, conditional statements, algebraic expressions, and simplifying linear expressions. Beyond standard fluency, students learn to analyze repeating digit cycles, place-value expansions in other bases, parity constraints, and necessary versus sufficient conditions.

The course covers linear equations and inequalities. Students solve and model with one-variable equations, rearrange multi-variable formulas, graph and interpret two-variable linear equations, solve systems of equations, and work with linear and compound inequalities. Special emphasis is placed on reasoning from incomplete or off-window graphs and optimizing expressions under given constraints.

The geometry portion of the course develops students’ understanding of points, lines, angles, transformations, polygons, solids, congruence, similarity, length, area, volume, circles, and radians. In addition to standard geometric theorems, students learn to reason about composite and inscribed figures, overlapping shapes, 3D solids reconstructed from 2D views and nets, and coordinate-geometry configurations.

The trigonometry portion of the course covers right-triangle trigonometry, the unit circle, special trigonometric ratios, general triangles, graphs and transformations of trigonometric functions, trigonometric identities, double-angle formulas, sum and difference formulas, and inverse trigonometric functions, including evaluating exact trigonometric ratios of inverse expressions.

Advanced algebra topics include functions, nested function compositions, graph transformations, absolute value constraints, polynomials, quadratic equations and functions, nonlinear systems, exponential and logarithmic equations, rational expressions, variable ratios from homogeneous equations, slant asymptotes, radical functions, complex numbers and nonreal zeros, nonstandard recurrences, arithmetic and geometric series, conic sections (including focus-distance properties of ellipses and hyperbolas), parametric and polar coordinates, vectors, and matrices.

Problem-solving, ratios, percentages, units, statistics, and probability are also covered. Students solve multi-stage problems involving ratios, unit rates, joint variation, percentages, and compound unit conversions, while developing statistical and probabilistic reasoning across frequency tables, data displays, measures of center and spread, data perturbations, correlation, conditional probability, combinatorics, and discrete random variables.
